# Limitations — Theorem I

1. **Gaussian/Hermite-Gaussian class only.** The theorem covers test functions
   even `P(z)e^{-az^2}` with real algebraic data. It does not cover general
   `C_c^∞` tests or general Paley-Wiener transforms.

2. **No compact-support claim.** The inverse Fourier transform of a Gaussian
   is Gaussian, not compactly supported. Thus this result does not solve the
   literal compact-support version of Paper A Open Problem 2.

3. **Nonvanishing is essential.** If the polynomial annihilates an on-line or
   off-line argument, the corresponding observation term disappears and a
   degenerate relation may exist.

4. **No approximate-collision bound.** Lindemann-Weierstrass is exact. It says
   nothing quantitative about small nonzero residuals or bounded-height
   approximate collisions.

5. **No analytic zero-set input.** All objects are finite formal multisets and
   finite observation vectors. No ordinate of a Riemann zero, zero-counting
   law, RH assumption, or RH-equivalent criterion is used.

6. **No universal encoding claim.** The result contrasts one rational encoding
   with one algebraic-exponential encoding. It does not classify all Weil-type
   observations or all information hierarchies.
