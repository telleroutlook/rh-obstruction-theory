\title[Finite Li-Statistic Collisions]{Finite
  Li-Statistic Collisions for Formal Symmetric Zero Multisets}

\author{Lin Tao}
\date{2026}

\begin{abstract}
We prove three theorems about the standard Li-type observation of finite
symmetric zero multisets, and record two main corollaries and one auxiliary corollary.
Let $\Hsym$ be the class of finite multisets in the open critical strip
symmetric under $\rho\mapsto\bar\rho$ and $\rho\mapsto 1-\rho$, and let
$P(\mathcal{Z})=1$ iff every atom has $\re\rho=1/2$.

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:A}} (exact Li collision): for every $m\geq 1$ there exist
$\mathcal{Z}_+,\mathcal{Z}_-\in\Hsym$ with $P(\mathcal{Z}_+)=1$,
$P(\mathcal{Z}_-)=0$, and identical standard Li observations
$\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+)=\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ for $j=1,\ldots,m$,
realised by explicit rational-arithmetic witnesses.

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:B}} (no uniform separation): no fixed finite initial
segment of standard Li values enforces a strictly positive gap between $P=1$ and $P=0$
within $\Hsym$.

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:C}} (incomparability): the standard Li map
$\Ofinite^{(K)}$ and the sampled counting map $\Otheta$ are
incomparable in the information refinement preorder on $\Hsym$.

\textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:D}} (Andersson): agreement with any fixed finite initial
collection of Euler factors of $\zeta$ does not force
a Helson zeta function's zeros to lie on the critical line
(existential obstruction pair, using Andersson~\cite{Andersson2024}
Theorem~5 and Corollary~3).

\textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:E}} (joint obstruction): the combined observation
$\Ofinite^{(K)}\times\Otheta$ also admits an exact information obstruction.

None of these results assume or imply the Riemann Hypothesis.
The class $\Hsym$ consists of \emph{finite} formal multisets;
the actual zero set of $\zeta$ is infinite and subject to additional
analytic structure not imposed on $\Hsym$.
A consequence is that $\Otheta$ alone does not determine $P$
(see Remark~\ref{rem:theta-not-P}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------

Li's criterion~\cite{Li1997} asserts that RH is equivalent to the
nonnegativity of all Li coefficients
$\lambda_n = \sum_\rho [1-(1-1/\rho)^n]$,
where $\rho$ runs over the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$ counted with
multiplicity, and the sum is taken in the symmetric sense
$\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{|\im\rho|\leq T}$
(Bombieri--Lagarias~\cite{BombieriLagarias1999}).
This reformulation raises a natural question: can one establish RH by
verifying, via a uniform template over all $n$, that $\lambda_n \geq 0$?
The results of this paper say that a strategy based on any \emph{fixed
finite initial segment} of Li values cannot decide the target predicate even on
the simplified class $\Hsym$ of finite symmetric multisets.  A proof scheme
that separately handles each $n$ but uses a uniform method reduces to the
full Li criterion and is not blocked.

\subsection*{What is an information obstruction?}

We work with an \emph{observation map} $O : X \to Y$, where $X$ is an
ambient class of formal zero configurations and $Y$ is a set of observable
records.  An \emph{information obstruction for $O$} is a
pair $(\mathcal{Z}_+, \mathcal{Z}_-) \in X^2$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $O(\mathcal{Z}_+) = O(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ (exact collision);
  \item $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) \neq P(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ (predicate separated);
  \item neither $\mathcal{Z}_+$ nor $\mathcal{Z}_-$ involves RH as a hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}
The obstruction is \emph{constructive} if the pair is explicitly computable;
otherwise it is \emph{existential}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} gives an exact information obstruction for $\Ofinite^{(m)}$
($X=\Hsym$, $O=\Ofinite^{(m)}$); Theorem~\ref{thm:B} is an approximation statement
(no uniform margin); Theorem~\ref{thm:C} concerns refinement incomparability between
$\Ofinite^{(K)}$ and $\Otheta$; in Corollary~\ref{cor:D}, $X=\mathcal{H}_S$ (the Helson class,
\S\ref{sec:helson}); Corollary~\ref{cor:E} gives an exact obstruction for the joint
map $\Ofinite^{(K)}\times\Otheta$.
An obstruction pair shows that no $f : Y \to \{0,1\}$ satisfies
$P(\mathcal{Z}) = f(O(\mathcal{Z}))$ for all $\mathcal{Z}\in\Hsym$.

\begin{remark}[Scope of these results]
Theorems~\ref{thm:A}--\ref{thm:C} and Corollary~\ref{cor:E} are proved for the class $\Hsym$ of
\emph{finite} symmetric multisets (Corollary~\ref{cor:D} concerns the Helson
class $\mathcal{H}_S$); none of these results apply directly to the
actual zero set of~$\zeta$.  The actual
zero set is infinite, satisfies the Riemann--von Mangoldt counting
asymptotic, and is governed by the Hadamard product, functional equation,
Gamma factor, and Euler product --- none of which are imposed on $\Hsym$.
Accordingly, the theorems say: a method that reads only $O(\mathcal{Z})$
for some $O \in \{\Ofinite^{(K)}, \Otheta\}$ cannot determine $P$ on
$\Hsym$.  They do not directly bound what a method using the full analytic
structure of $\zeta$ can achieve.
An information obstruction does not prevent RH from being true, nor does it
prevent an RH proof that uses structure not captured by $O$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Analogy with complexity-theoretic barriers]
The closest structural analogues are the Razborov--Rudich natural
proofs barrier~\cite{RazborovRudich1997} and the relativization
barrier~\cite{BakerGillSolovay1975}: each exhibits adversarial witnesses
showing a class of proof strategies cannot decide a target predicate.
The analogy is structural and motivational, not a formal reduction;
those frameworks involve constructivity, density hypotheses, and
diagonalization that are absent here.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Results}

The ambient class $\Hsym$ consists of finite zero multisets in the open
critical strip $\{0 < \re \rho < 1\}$, closed under $\rho \mapsto \bar\rho$
and $\rho \mapsto 1 - \rho$ (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).  The target
predicate is $P(\mathcal{Z}) = 1$ iff every atom has $\re \rho = 1/2$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:A}} (Section~\ref{sec:B2}): For every fixed $m\geq 1$,
  there exist $\mathcal{Z}_+, \mathcal{Z}_- \in \Hsym$ with
  $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 1$, $P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$, and
  $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ exactly for $j=1,\ldots,m$.

\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:B}} (Section~\ref{sec:B1}): No fixed finite collection
  of standard Li values admits a uniform strictly positive lower bound
  separating $P(\mathcal{Z}) = 1$ from $P(\mathcal{Z}) = 0$ within $\Hsym$.

\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:C}} (Section~\ref{sec:H}): Fix $M \geq 1$ and
  positive sampling levels $0 < d_1 < \cdots < d_M$.  For every $K \geq 1$,
  $\Ofinite^{(K)} \incomp \Otheta$ on $\Hsym$.

\item \textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:D}} (Section~\ref{sec:C}): For every finite prime
  cutoff $P_0$ and every $z_1 \in S$ with $\re z_1 \neq 1/2$, there
  exists a Helson zeta function agreeing with $\zeta$ on all Euler
  factors $p \leq P_0$ yet having a zero at $z_1$; and there exists one
  agreeing on all Euler factors $p \leq P_0$ that is zero-free in $S$
  (hence $P_S = 1$ vacuously).

\item \textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:E}} (Section~\ref{sec:H}): The joint observation
  $\Ofinite^{(K)} \times \Otheta$ also has an exact information obstruction
  on $\Hsym$.
\end{itemize}

All results are unconditional.

\subsection*{Comparison with prior work}

\textbf{Li's criterion and reformulations.}
Li's criterion~\cite{Li1997} and Bombieri--Lagarias~\cite{BombieriLagarias1999}
give RH-\emph{equivalences}: they locate difficulty but do not block proof
methods.  Bombieri--Lagarias (Corollary~1 in~\cite{BombieriLagarias1999},
under the conjugate and $s\mapsto 1-s$ symmetry)
show that $\lambda_n \geq 0$ for all $n$ is equivalent to RH;
the present paper works in the more
constrained symmetric class $\Hsym$ and shows that \emph{finite} truncations
$(\Li_1,\ldots,\Li_m)$ are non-identifying within this class.

\textbf{Weil explicit formula and positivity.}
The Weil explicit formula expresses sums over zeros as sums over primes
plus archimedean contributions; positivity of these sums for all test
functions would imply RH.  Connes~\cite{ConnesWeilFF1999} and
Bombieri~\cite{Bombieri2000} reformulate this as a positivity and
spectral-absorption problem.  These approaches use \emph{all} test functions
simultaneously; the present results show that, within the finite symmetric
class $\Hsym$, a \emph{fixed finite initial segment} of standard Li values
does not determine $P$.  The case of a fixed finite family of general
Weil-type tests is addressed by Open Problem~2.

\textbf{Selberg trace formula and spectral methods.}
The Selberg trace formula connects eigenvalues to geodesic lengths;
see Hejhal~\cite{Hejhal1976}.  Connes--Consani--Moscovici~\cite{CCM2025}
construct zeta spectral triples whose Dirac-operator regularized determinant
satisfies (Theorem~5.10, under the hypothesis that the lowest eigenvalue of
the finite-truncation Weil form~$QW_\lambda^N$ is simple with even eigenvector,
normalized by $\delta_N(\xi_{\lambda,N})=1$; self-adjointness is a conclusion
of the theorem)
$\det_{\mathrm{reg}}(D_{\log}^{(\lambda,N)}-z)=-i\lambda^{-iz}\hat{\xi}_{\lambda,N}(z)$.
The convergence $\hat{k}_\lambda\to\Xi$ for $|\operatorname{Im}z|<\tfrac12$
is established in Lemma~7.3 of~\cite{CCM2025}.  The open steps named in \S8 of~\cite{CCM2025} are: (i)~establishing that
the lowest eigenvalue of the continuous $QW_\lambda$ is simple with even
eigenfunction; and (ii)~verifying that $k_\lambda$ approximates the
corresponding normalized lowest eigenfunction sufficiently accurately to
recover~$\Xi$.  The decomposition of step~(i) into a finite-$N$
simplicity/evenness claim and an $N\to\infty$ continuum limit is the
authors' refinement, not an explicit item in~\S8.

\textbf{Helson class and Dirichlet series.}
Helson~\cite{Helson1969} introduced random Dirichlet series with unimodular
coefficients; zero distribution was studied by Seip~\cite{Seip2020} and
Bochkov--Romanov~\cite{BochkovRomanov2022}.
Andersson~\cite{Andersson2024} proved that prescribed zeros in the critical
strip can be realized by Helson zeta functions; Corollary~\ref{cor:D} makes the
finite-Euler-factor limitation explicit as an existential obstruction.

\textbf{Rational arithmetic witnesses.}
The exact rational witnesses for the $m=2$ case have been verified by
independent computation using exact rational arithmetic
(\texttt{fractions.Fraction} in Python).

\section{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Ambient class}

\begin{definition}[Ambient class $\Hsym$]
\label{def:ambient}
A finite multiset $\mathcal{Z}$ of complex numbers is in $\Hsym$ if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(S\arabic*)}]
  \item\label{itm:strip} every atom satisfies $0 < \re\rho < 1$;
  \item\label{itm:conj} $\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$ implies $\bar\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$
    (same multiplicity);
  \item $\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$ implies $1-\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$
    (same multiplicity).
\end{enumerate}
The target predicate is $P(\mathcal{Z}) = 1$ iff $\re\rho = 1/2$ for every
atom $\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$.
\end{definition}

Condition~\ref{itm:strip} excludes $0$ and $1$, ensuring that
$\varphi_j(\rho)$ is well-defined for all standard Li tests.
The class $\Hsym$ consists of \emph{finite} formal multisets; the actual
zero set of $\zeta$ is infinite and does not belong to $\Hsym$.

\subsection{Standard Li tests and the finite observation map}

\begin{definition}[Standard Li tests]
\label{def:Listd}
For $j \geq 1$, the \emph{$j$-th standard Li test} is
\[
  \varphi_j(\rho) = 1 - \bigl(1 - \rho^{-1}\bigr)^j.
\]
For $\sigma\in(0,1)$ and $|t|\geq 1$,
$|\varphi_j(\sigma+it)| = j/|\sigma+it| + O(|t|^{-2}) = O(|t|^{-1})$
uniformly in $\sigma$, but not $O(|t|^{-1-\delta})$ for any $\delta>0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Other test families]
For moment-type tests $\varphi_j(\rho)=\rho^{-k_j}$ (with specified distinct
exponents $k_j$), distinct heights $t_k$ do not by themselves imply
nonsingularity of the matrix $[4\Re((1/2+it_k)^{-k_j})]$: the nodes
$\re\rho_k = 1/2$ are all equal and a standard Vandermonde argument
does not apply.  Invertibility must be verified separately for each
choice of exponents (see Remark~\ref{rem:momentVander}).
For test functions $h\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$, the Paley--Wiener theorem
implies that the Fourier transform $\hat h$ extends to an entire function
of $z\in\CC$, so $\varphi_h(\rho)=\hat h((\rho-\tfrac12)/i)$ is an
entire (hence meromorphic) function of $\rho$.  The difficulty is not
meromorphicity but rather the non-algebraic structure and the absence of
rationality for the resulting observation values; their treatment is
deferred.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Rescaled Li observation and finite observation map]
\label{def:Li}
For $\mathcal{Z} \in \Hsym$ and $j \geq 1$, define
\[
  \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}) = 2 \sum_{\rho \in \mathcal{Z}} \varphi_j(\rho).
\]
Here $\varphi_j$ is the standard Li test from Definition~\ref{def:Listd}.
This sum is real.  Indeed, condition~\ref{itm:conj}
implies that $\mathcal{Z}$ is closed under complex conjugation
($\rho\mapsto\bar\rho$), so the sum comes in conjugate orbits:
non-real atoms pair as $\{\rho,\bar\rho\}$, contributing
$\varphi_j(\rho)+\varphi_j(\bar\rho) = 2\re\varphi_j(\rho) \in \RR$
(using $\varphi_j(\bar\rho)=\overline{\varphi_j(\rho)}$, which follows
from $\bar\rho^{-1}=\overline{\rho^{-1}}$);
real atoms satisfy $\bar\rho=\rho$, so $\varphi_j(\rho)\in\RR$ directly
and each contributes $2\varphi_j(\rho)\in\RR$ as a self-conjugate orbit.

The \emph{finite observation map} with $K$ tests is
\[
  \Ofinite^{(K)} : \Hsym \longrightarrow \RR^K, \qquad
  \Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}) = \bigl(\Li_1(\mathcal{Z}), \ldots, \Li_K(\mathcal{Z})\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Normalization convention]
The factor of $2$ in Definition~\ref{def:Li} is a non-standard rescaling
(``R-symm'', a non-standard rescaling introduced here): the standard
convention sums $\varphi_j$ once per atom (``R-atom''), giving half the
value.  Every qualitative conclusion (existence of collisions, absence of
uniform gaps) is convention-independent; all numerical anchors use R-symm.
The classical Li coefficients $\lambda_n$ of $\zeta$ are defined via a
symmetric limit over the actual infinite zero set and should not be
confused with $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z})$ for finite $\mathcal{Z}\in\Hsym$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sampled counting map}

\begin{definition}[Sampled counting map]
\label{def:Otheta}
Fix $M \geq 1$ and positive sampling levels $0 < d_1 < \cdots < d_M$.
For $\mathcal{Z} \in \Hsym$, let
$\NZ(u) = \#\{\rho \in \mathcal{Z} : |\im\rho| \leq u\}$
(with multiplicity).
The \emph{sampled counting map} is
\[
  \Otheta : \Hsym \longrightarrow \ZZ_{\geq 0}^M, \qquad
  \Otheta(\mathcal{Z}) = \bigl(\NZ(d_1), \ldots, \NZ(d_M)\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

The map $\Otheta$ is nonconstant and depends on $\mathcal{Z}$.

\subsection{Information refinement preorder}

\begin{definition}[Refinement preorder]
\label{def:preorder}
For two observation maps $O_a, O_b : \Hsym \to S_a, S_b$, write
$O_a \preceq O_b$ if there exists $f$ with $O_a = f \circ O_b$ on $\Hsym$
(``$O_b$ refines $O_a$'').  Write $O_a \incomp O_b$ for
\emph{incomparability}: $O_a \npreceq O_b$ and $O_b \npreceq O_a$.
\end{definition}

This is a preorder (reflexive and transitive) but not antisymmetric in
general.  No lattice structure is claimed.

\subsection{Building blocks: the on-line pair and the off-line quartet}

Two basic building blocks recur throughout:
\begin{align*}
  L(t) &= \{{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}} + it,\; {\textstyle\frac{1}{2}} - it\}
         && \text{(critical-line pair, }t > 0\text{)},\\
  Q(\sigma_0, T) &= \{\sigma_0 \pm iT,\; 1-\sigma_0 \pm iT\}
         && \text{(off-line quartet, }\sigma_0 \neq {\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\text{)}.
\end{align*}
Both are in $\Hsym$ for $0 < \sigma_0 < 1$, $t, T > 0$.
We have $P(L(t)) = 1$ and $P(Q(\sigma_0,T)) = 0$ for $\sigma_0 \neq 1/2$.
All collections above are interpreted as multisets; in particular, $Q(1/2,T) = 2L(T)$.

Direct computation using Definition~\ref{def:Li} gives:
\begin{align}
  \Li_j(L(t)) &= 4\,\re\varphi_j\!\bigl({\textstyle\frac12}+it\bigr),
                \label{eq:Lionline}\\
  \Li_j(Q(\sigma_0,T))
    &= 4\,\re\bigl[\varphi_j(\sigma_0+iT)
       + \varphi_j(1{-}\sigma_0+iT)\bigr].
    \label{eq:Lioffl}
\end{align}

\subsection{Sum-product identity and quartet asymptotics}

\begin{lemma}[Sum-product identity~{\cite{BombieriLagarias1999}}]
For all $j \geq 1$ and $s \in \CC \setminus \{0,1\}$:
\[
  \varphi_j(s) + \varphi_j(1-s) \;=\; \varphi_j(s)\cdot\varphi_j(1-s).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $A=(1-s^{-1})^j$ and $B=(1-(1-s)^{-1})^j$.
Since $(1-s^{-1})(1-(1-s)^{-1}) = \tfrac{s-1}{s}\cdot\tfrac{-s}{1-s} = 1$,
we have $AB = [(1-s^{-1})(1-(1-s)^{-1})]^j = 1$.  Hence
\begin{align*}
  \varphi_j(s)\cdot\varphi_j(1-s)
  &= (1-A)(1-B) = 1 - A - B + AB = 2 - A - B\\
  &= \varphi_j(s) + \varphi_j(1-s).\qedhere
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

% Original result; independently verified in theorems/B2-exact-collision/checker/b2_certified_checker.py
\begin{lemma}[Quartet Li asymptotics]
\label{lem:qasympt}
For each fixed $j\ge 1$ and every $\sigma_0\in(0,1)$, as $T\to+\infty$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:djasympt}
  q_j(T) \;:=\; \Li_j(Q(\sigma_0,T)) \;=\; \frac{4j^2}{T^2} + O_j(T^{-4}),
\end{equation}
uniformly in $\sigma_0\in(0,1)$ (the implied constant depends on $j$).
In particular, $q_j(T) \to 0$ for each fixed $j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:Lioffl},
$q_j(T) = 4\re[\varphi_j(\sigma_0+iT) + \varphi_j(1-\sigma_0+iT)]$.
Set $s_1 = \sigma_0+iT$ and $s_2 = 1-\sigma_0+iT$.
For each $s_k = \sigma_k + iT$ ($\sigma_1=\sigma_0$, $\sigma_2=1-\sigma_0$),
expanding $1/s_k = \sigma_k/T^2 - i/T + O(T^{-3})$ gives
\[
  \varphi_j(s_k) = 1 - (1-s_k^{-1})^j
  = -\tfrac{ij}{T} + \tfrac{j(\sigma_k + j/2 - 1/2)}{T^2} + O(T^{-3}).
\]
Summing over $k=1,2$ and using $\sigma_1+\sigma_2 = 1$:
\[
  \varphi_j(s_1) + \varphi_j(s_2)
  = -\tfrac{2ij}{T} + \tfrac{j^2}{T^2} + O(T^{-3}).
\]
Set $g(T) = \varphi_j(s_1)+\varphi_j(s_2)$.
Since $\varphi_j$ has real coefficients, $\varphi_j(\bar{s}) = \overline{\varphi_j(s)}$,
hence $g(-T) = \overline{g(T)}$.  Writing $g(T) = \sum_n c_n T^{-n}$ as an analytic
function of $u=1/T$, the symmetry $g(-T)=\overline{g(T)}$ forces
$c_n(-1)^n = \bar c_n$: odd-indexed coefficients are purely imaginary and
even-indexed are real.  The expansion to the relevant order is therefore
\[
  g(T) = -\tfrac{2ij}{T} + \tfrac{j^2}{T^2} + \tfrac{ic_3}{T^3} + \tfrac{c_4}{T^4}
  + O(T^{-5}), \qquad c_3, c_4 \in \RR,
\]
so $\re[g(T)] = j^2/T^2 + c_4/T^4 + O(T^{-5}) = j^2/T^2 + O(T^{-4})$
and $q_j(T) = 4j^2/T^2 + O(T^{-4})$.
Since $\varphi_j(\sigma_0+iT)$ is jointly analytic in $(\sigma_0,u)$ for
$\sigma_0\in[0,1]$ and $u=1/T$ near~$0$, the remainder $O(T^{-4})$ is
uniform in $\sigma_0\in[0,1]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:q1sign}
The main term $4j^2/T^2$ is independent of $\sigma_0$.  For $j=1$:
\[
  q_1(T) = 4\Bigl[\frac{\sigma_0}{\sigma_0^2+T^2}
            + \frac{1-\sigma_0}{(1-\sigma_0)^2+T^2}\Bigr] > 0
\]
for all $T>0$, since both terms are positive.
For $j\geq 2$, $q_j(T)>0$ for all sufficiently large $T$ (following from
$q_j(T) = 4j^2/T^2 + O(T^{-4})$), but this need not hold for small $T$:
for example, $q_2(1/10) < 0$ at $\sigma_0 = 3/4$
(verified by exact rational arithmetic in
\texttt{checker/q2\_sign\_check.py}).
\end{remark}

\section{Li observations admit exact collisions (Theorem~\ref{thm:A})}
\label{sec:B2}

\subsection{Statement}

\begin{mainthm}[Exact Li collision]
\label{thm:A}
For every $m \geq 1$ there exist $\mathcal{Z}_+, \mathcal{Z}_- \in \Hsym$
with $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 1$, $P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$, and
\[
  \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) \qquad
  \text{for all } j = 1, \ldots, m,
\]
where $\Li_j$ uses the standard Li tests $\varphi_j(\rho)=1-(1-\rho^{-1})^j$.
Moreover, $\mathcal{Z}_\pm$ can be taken to have rational atom-heights and
all multiplicities nonnegative integers.  For any $H>0$, the parameters
can be chosen so that all atoms of $\mathcal{Z}_\pm$ have $|\im\rho|>H$.
\end{mainthm}

\subsection{The collision matrix}

Fix distinct positive rationals $0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_m$ and a rational
$T > t_m$.  Let $\mathcal{Z}_+$ consist of $B$ copies of each $L(t_k)$
(for a buffer $B \geq 1$ to be chosen), and let $\mathcal{Z}_-$ contain
$(B + n_k)$ copies of $L(t_k)$ and $R$ copies of $Q(3/4, T)$,
where $n_k \in \ZZ$ with $B + n_k \geq 0$.

The collision condition $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-)$
becomes, for $j = 1, \ldots, m$:
\[
  \sum_{k=1}^m n_k \, C_{jk} + R \, q_j(T) = 0,
  \tag{$\star$}\label{eq:collision}
\]
where
\begin{align*}
  C_{jk} &= \Li_j(L(t_k)) = 4\,\re\varphi_j\!\bigl({\textstyle\frac12}+it_k\bigr),\\
  q_j(T) &= \Li_j(Q(3/4,T))
           = 4\,\re\bigl[\varphi_j({\textstyle\frac34}+iT)
             + \varphi_j({\textstyle\frac14}+iT)\bigr].
\end{align*}
In matrix form, $Cn + Rq(T) = 0$ where $C \in \RR^{m \times m}$
and $q(T) \in \RR^m$.

\subsection{Non-singularity of the Li collision matrix}

% Original result; proof self-contained via Chebyshev identity and Vandermonde determinant
\begin{lemma}[Full rank of $C$]
\label{lem:Crank}
For distinct positive real numbers $t_1, \ldots, t_m$, the matrix
$C = (C_{jk})$ with $C_{jk} = 4(1 - T_j(x_k))$,
where $x_k = (4t_k^2 - 1)/(4t_k^2+1)$ and $T_j$ is the
degree-$j$ Chebyshev polynomial, satisfies $\det C \neq 0$.
If moreover $t_k \in \QQ$, then $C \in \QQ^{m\times m}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Step 1: derive $C_{jk} = 4(1-T_j(x_k))$.}
By~\eqref{eq:Lionline}, $C_{jk} = 4\,\re\varphi_j(\rho_k)$ where $\rho_k = \tfrac12+it_k$.
Let $w_k = 1 - \rho_k^{-1}$ where $\rho_k = \tfrac12 + it_k$.
Then
\[
  |w_k|^2 = \frac{(t_k^2 - 1/4)^2 + t_k^2}{(1/4+t_k^2)^2}
           = \frac{(t_k^2+1/4)^2}{(t_k^2+1/4)^2} = 1,
\]
so $w_k$ lies on the unit circle with $\re w_k = (4t_k^2-1)/(4t_k^2+1) = x_k$.
Since $|w_k|=1$, the Chebyshev identity $\re(z^j)=T_j(\re z)$ for $|z|=1$ gives
$\re w_k^j = T_j(x_k)$.  Hence
$\re\varphi_j(\rho_k) = \re(1-w_k^j) = 1-T_j(x_k)$,
so $C_{jk} = 4(1-T_j(x_k))$.

\textbf{Step 2: rationality and non-singularity.}
Since $t_k \in \QQ$, each $x_k \in \QQ$; since $T_j$ has integer
coefficients, $C_{jk} = 4(1-T_j(x_k)) \in \QQ$.  For non-singularity:
$T_j(1) = 1$ gives $1 - T_j(x) = (1-x)\,r_{j-1}(x)$ for a polynomial $r_{j-1}$
of degree exactly $j-1$ with leading coefficient $2^{j-1}$.  Thus
$C_{jk} = 4(1-x_k)\,r_{j-1}(x_k)$,
and $C = 4 \cdot [r_{j-1}(x_k)] \cdot \operatorname{diag}(1-x_k)$.
Since $x_k \in (-1,1)$, each $1-x_k > 0$, so $\det C \neq 0$ iff
$\det[r_{j-1}(x_k)] \neq 0$.  The polynomials $\{r_{j-1}\}_{j=1}^m$ have
degrees $0,1,\ldots,m-1$ with positive leading coefficients, so
$[r_{j-1}(x_k)] = A \cdot V(x_1, \ldots, x_m)$ where $A$ is lower-triangular
with positive diagonal entries and $V$ is the Vandermonde matrix.
The $x_k$ are distinct (since $x(t)=(4t^2-1)/(4t^2+1)$ is strictly
increasing on $(0,\infty)$), so $\det V \neq 0$, hence $\det C \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:momentVander}
For moment-type tests $\varphi_j(\rho) = \rho^{-j}$ (consecutive integer
exponents), set $z_k = \rho_k^{-1}$ and write
$r_k = |z_k| = 2/\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}$ and $\alpha_k = \arctan(2t_k)$, so
\[
  C_{jk} = 4\re(\rho_k^{-j}) = 4\,r_k^j\cos(j\alpha_k).
\]
Unlike the standard Li case, where $w_k = 1-\rho_k^{-1}$ lies exactly on the
unit circle and the Chebyshev identity $\re(z^j)=T_j(\re z)$ applies for
$|z|=1$, here $r_k$ is generally not equal to~$1$ ($r_k<1$ iff $t_k>\sqrt{3}/2$,
e.g.\ $r_k=\sqrt{2}>1$ when $t_k=1/2$), and the $j$-dependent factor $r_k^j$ cannot be
absorbed into a Chebyshev evaluation; the reduction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:Crank} does not apply.  The matrix $[C_{jk}] = 4[\re(z_k^j)]$
is the real part of the Vandermonde matrix in $z_1,\ldots,z_m$; whether
it is non-singular for given nodes $z_k = \rho_k^{-1}$ requires a separate
argument and does not follow from ``distinct $\cos\alpha_k$'' alone.
For non-consecutive exponents $\{k_j\}$, the matrix is the real part of a
sub-Vandermonde evaluated at $z_k$ (i.e.\ $C_{jk}=4\operatorname{Re}(z_k^{k_j})$);
taking the real part may destroy invertibility, so non-singularity requires a separate
verification for each choice of exponents and nodes.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Rational solution and nonnegative integer multiplicities}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
Choose distinct rational $t_1 < \cdots < t_m$ and rational $T > t_m$;
for the $H>0$ clause, choose all $t_k, T > H$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:Crank}, $C \in \QQ^{m\times m}$ with $\det C \neq 0$.
When $T \in \QQ$, each $q_j(T)$ is rational (sum of rational functions
evaluated at rational $T$), so $q(T) \in \QQ^m$.  Set
\[
  \beta = -C^{-1} q(T) \in \QQ^m.
\]
Since $q_1(T) > 0$ for all $T>0$ (exact formula in
Remark~\ref{rem:q1sign}, both terms positive), $q(T) \neq 0$,
so $\beta \neq 0$.
Let $R = \operatorname{lcm}(\text{denominators of } \beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m)
\geq 1$ and $n_k = R\beta_k \in \ZZ$.
Then $Cn + Rq(T) = 0$, i.e.\ \eqref{eq:collision} holds.

Set $B = \max_k |n_k| \geq 1$.  Then $B + n_k \geq 0$ for all~$k$.
Let $\mathcal{Z}_+$ contain $B$ copies of $L(t_k)$ ($k=1,\ldots,m$),
and $\mathcal{Z}_-$ contain $(B + n_k)$ copies of $L(t_k)$ plus $R$
copies of $Q(3/4, T)$.  Both are in $\Hsym$ (Definition~\ref{def:ambient}); all multiplicities are
$\geq 0$.  By construction, $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-)$
for $j=1,\ldots,m$, $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 1$, and $P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$
(atoms at $\re\rho = 3/4$).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Numerical illustration (\texorpdfstring{$m=2$}{m=2}, exact rational arithmetic)}

We exhibit the complete collision for $m=2$ with parameters
$t_1=1$, $t_2=2$, $T=3$, $\sigma_0=3/4$.

\textbf{Chebyshev nodes.}
$x_k = (4t_k^2-1)/(4t_k^2+1)$:
$x_1 = \tfrac{3}{5}$, $x_2 = \tfrac{15}{17}$.

\textbf{Collision matrix.}  $C_{jk} = 4(1-T_j(x_k))$, $T_1(x)=x$, $T_2(x)=2x^2-1$:
\[
  C = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{8}{5} & \tfrac{8}{17} \\[4pt]
                      \tfrac{128}{25} & \tfrac{512}{289} \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \det C = \tfrac{3072}{7225} \neq 0.
\]

\textbf{Quartet tail.}  $q_j = \Li_j(Q(3/4, 3))$ via \eqref{eq:Lioffl}:
\[
  q_1 = \tfrac{3136}{7395}, \qquad q_2 = \tfrac{10041088}{6076225}.
\]

\textbf{Rational solution.}  $\beta = -C^{-1}q$:
\[
  \beta_1 = \tfrac{7978}{128673}, \qquad
  \beta_2 = -\tfrac{42082}{37845}.
\]
Clearing denominators: $R = \mathrm{lcm}(128673,\, 37845) = 643365$, giving
$n_1 = R\beta_1 = 39890$, $n_2 = R\beta_2 = -715394$.
Buffer: $B = 715394$, so $B+n_1 = 755284 \geq 0$ and $B+n_2 = 0 \geq 0$.

\textbf{Exact zero residual} (verified by \texttt{fractions.Fraction}):
$C_{j1}n_1 + C_{j2}n_2 + R\,q_j = 0$ for $j=1,2$.

\textbf{Collision pair.}  $\mathcal{Z}_+$ contains $B = 715394$ copies of
$L(1)$ and $L(2)$; $\mathcal{Z}_-$ contains $755284$ copies of $L(1)$,
$0$ copies of $L(2)$, and $R = 643365$ copies of $Q(3/4, 3)$.
Both have $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ exactly for $j=1,2$,
and $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 1$, $P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$.

\section{No uniform Li separation (Theorem~\ref{thm:B})}
\label{sec:B1}

\begin{mainthm}[No uniform separation]
\label{thm:B}
Fix $m \geq 1$ and $c_1,\ldots,c_m \in \RR$.  Let $\mathcal{Z}_+ \in \Hsym$
satisfy $P(\mathcal{Z}_+)=1$ and $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) > c_j$ for each
$j=1,\ldots,m$.  Then for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists
$\mathcal{Z}_- \in \Hsym$ with $P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$ and
$|\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) - \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+)| < \varepsilon$
for all $j = 1, \ldots, m$.
In particular, $\mathcal{Z}_-$ satisfies $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) > c_j$ for all $j$
whenever
$0 < \varepsilon < \min_{1 \leq j \leq m}\bigl(\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) - c_j\bigr)$.
\end{mainthm}

\begin{proof}
Set $\mathcal{Z}_- = \mathcal{Z}_+ \sqcup Q(3/4, T)$ for large $T$.  Then
$\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) + q_j(T)$, where
$q_j(T) = 4j^2/T^2 + O_j(T^{-4}) \to 0$ by~\eqref{eq:djasympt} and Lemma~\ref{lem:qasympt}.
Choose $T$ large enough that $|q_j(T)| < \varepsilon$ for all $j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$
Since $Q(3/4,T)$ contributes atoms at $\re\rho = 3/4 \neq 1/2$,
$P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Approximate vs.\ exact obstruction]
Theorems~A and~B have different quantifier structures; neither statement
is an immediate consequence of the other.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} constructs one specific pair
$(\mathcal{Z}_+, \mathcal{Z}_-)$ with exactly equal Li values; it does
\emph{not} assert that every $P=1$ witness admits an exact collision partner.
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} asserts that for \emph{any} given $P=1$ witness $\mathcal{Z}_+$,
a $P=0$ object can be found approximating all $m$ Li values to within~$\varepsilon$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} additionally uses the $\QQ$-rationality of $C$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:Crank}) to achieve $\varepsilon=0$ for a specific pair;
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} requires only $q_j(T)\to 0$.
The escape from both theorems is an unbounded hierarchy $m\to\infty$,
or imposing the full Euler product, functional equation, or
Riemann--von Mangoldt counting asymptotics.
\end{remark}

% Auxiliary corollary; requires Theorem~\ref{thm:B} (given any $\mathcal{Z}_+$, construct a matching $\mathcal{Z}_-$)
\begin{corollary}[Positivity threshold]
\label{cor:B1Li}
Fix $K\ge 1$.
Let $\mathcal{Z}_+ \in \Hsym$ satisfy $P(\mathcal{Z}_+)=1$ and
$\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) > 0$ for $j=1,\ldots,K$.  Then there exists
$\mathcal{Z}_- \in \Hsym$ with $P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$ and
$\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) > 0$ for $j=1,\ldots,K$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:B} with $c_j = 0$ for all $j = 1,\ldots,K$ to the given
$\mathcal{Z}_+$ with
$\varepsilon = \tfrac12\min_{1\le j\le K}\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) > 0$
to obtain $\mathcal{Z}_-$ with $P(\mathcal{Z}_-)=0$ and
$\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) > \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+)/2 > 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Not every $\mathcal{Z}_+\in\Hsym$ with $P(\mathcal{Z}_+)=1$ satisfies
$\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+)>0$ for all $j$: for instance
$\varphi_4(1/2+i/2) = 1 - (1-(1/2+i/2)^{-1})^4 = 1 - i^4 = 0$,
so $\Li_4(L(1/2)) = 0$.  The corollary applies only to $\mathcal{Z}_+$
where the positivity hypothesis holds.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Setting: Helson zeta functions}
\label{sec:helson}

For a completely multiplicative unimodular function
$\chi : \mathbb{N} \to \TT$ ($|\chi(p)| = 1$ for all primes $p$), define
\[
  \zeta_\chi(s) = \sum_{n \geq 1} \chi(n) n^{-s}
               = \prod_p (1-\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1},
\]
convergent for $\re s > 1$.  For a finite prime cutoff $P_0$, say $\chi$
is \emph{$P_0$-standard} if $\chi(p) = 1$ for all primes $p \leq P_0$.

Set $U_0 := \{\re s > 0\}$.  Let $\mathcal{H}_S$ denote the class of
Helson zeta functions $\zeta_\chi$ that admit a single-valued meromorphic
continuation to the fixed half-plane $U_0$ (both constructions in
Corollary~\ref{cor:D} satisfy this condition).  Set $S := \{0 < \re s < 1\}$
and define the target predicate $P_S(\zeta_\chi) = 1$ iff every zero
$\rho \in S$ of $\zeta_\chi$ satisfies $\re\rho = 1/2$.

\begin{remark}
The Helson coefficients satisfy $|\chi(n)| = 1$ for all $n$, which
is a very strong (in fact Ramanujan-type) bound.  Nevertheless the Helson
class does not require a functional equation, gamma factors, or a finite
analytic conductor; it therefore does not carry full Selberg-class
structure.  The escape route for Corollary~\ref{cor:D} is any proof method that
uses the functional equation, Gamma factors, or the Selberg-class axioms.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Statement}

\begin{maincor}[Finite Euler factors do not force critical-line zeros]
\label{cor:D}
For every finite prime cutoff $P_0$ and every $z_1 \in S$ with
$\re z_1 \neq 1/2$, there exist:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item a completely multiplicative unimodular $\tilde\chi$ that is
    $P_0$-standard, such that $\zeta_{\tilde\chi} \in \mathcal{H}_S$
    and $P_S(\zeta_{\tilde\chi}) = 0$;
  \item a completely multiplicative unimodular $\chi^+$,
    also $P_0$-standard, such that $\zeta_{\chi^+} \in \mathcal{H}_S$
    and $P_S(\zeta_{\chi^+}) = 1$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the pair $(\zeta_{\chi^+}, \zeta_{\tilde\chi})$ is an
existential information obstruction for the finite-Euler-factor observation
$O_{P_0}(\zeta_\chi) = (\chi(p))_{p \leq P_0}$.
\end{maincor}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Part (a): constructing $P_S=0$.}

By Andersson~\cite[Theorem~5]{Andersson2024} applied with $U = \{\re s > 0\}$
and the signed divisor $D = [z_1]$ (a simple zero at $z_1$, $\re z_1 \neq 1/2$),
there exists a completely
multiplicative unimodular $\chi$ such that $\zeta_\chi$ extends
meromorphically to $U_0=\{\re s>0\}$ with a simple zero at $z_1$.
Define
\[
  \tilde\chi(p) =
    \begin{cases} 1 & p \leq P_0, \\ \chi(p) & p > P_0, \end{cases}
\]
extended completely multiplicatively.  Then $\tilde\chi$ is
$P_0$-standard.  For $\re s > 1$:
\[
  \zeta_{\tilde\chi}(s) = \zeta_\chi(s) \cdot
    \prod_{p \leq P_0} \frac{1 - \chi(p)p^{-s}}{1 - p^{-s}}
  =: \zeta_\chi(s) \cdot R(s).
\]
Since $|\chi(p)| = 1$ and $|p^{-s}| = p^{-\re s}$, the zeros
of $1-\chi(p)p^{-s}$ satisfy $|p^{-s}|=1$, i.e.\ $\re s = 0$;
similarly the zeros of $1-p^{-s}$ satisfy $\re s = 0$.
So $R$ extends to a holomorphic, nonvanishing function on
$\{\re s > 0\} \supset S$.  Thus $\zeta_{\tilde\chi} = \zeta_\chi \cdot R$
extends meromorphically to $U_0$, so $\zeta_{\tilde\chi}\in\mathcal{H}_S$.
Since $R(z_1) \neq 0$, $\zeta_{\tilde\chi}$
has a zero at $z_1$, hence $P_S(\zeta_{\tilde\chi}) = 0$.

\textbf{Part (b): constructing $P_S=1$.}

By Andersson~\cite[Corollary~3]{Andersson2024}, there exists a completely
multiplicative unimodular $\chi_0$ such that $\zeta_{\chi_0}$ extends
to an entire function on $\CC$ with no zeros in $S$.  Apply the same
finite-factor modification:
\[
  \chi^+(p) =
    \begin{cases} 1 & p \leq P_0, \\ \chi_0(p) & p > P_0, \end{cases}
\]
extended completely multiplicatively.  Then $\chi^+$ is $P_0$-standard,
and
$\zeta_{\chi^+} = \zeta_{\chi_0} \cdot R_0$
where $R_0(s) = \prod_{p\le P_0}(1-\chi_0(p)p^{-s})/(1-p^{-s})$.
By the same Euler-factor argument as part~(a), the zeros of each factor
$(1-\chi_0(p)p^{-s})$ and $(1-p^{-s})$ satisfy $\re s = 0$, so $R_0$
extends to a holomorphic, nonvanishing function on $\{\re s > 0\} = U_0$.
Since $\zeta_{\chi_0}$ is entire (hence extends holomorphically to $U_0$),
$\zeta_{\chi^+} = \zeta_{\chi_0}\cdot R_0$ extends holomorphically to $U_0$,
so $\zeta_{\chi^+}\in\mathcal{H}_S$.
Since $\zeta_{\chi_0}$ has no zeros in $S$ and $R_0$ has none either,
$\zeta_{\chi^+}$ has no zeros in $S$, hence $P_S(\zeta_{\chi^+}) = 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Corollary~\ref{cor:D} is an \emph{existential} obstruction: Andersson's theorem
gives existence but not explicit values of $\chi(p)$.  The pair
$(\zeta_{\chi^+}, \zeta_{\tilde\chi})$ exists but is not constructive.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
In part~(b), $\zeta_{\chi^+}$ has no zeros in $S$ by construction;
$P_S(\zeta_{\chi^+}) = 1$ holds vacuously (the zero set in $S$ is empty).
The obstruction remains valid: $O_{P_0}$ cannot distinguish
$\zeta_{\tilde\chi}$ (with a zero off the critical line) from
$\zeta_{\chi^+}$ (zero-free in $S$) when their Euler factors at all
primes $p \leq P_0$ agree.
\end{remark}

\section{Li and counting observations are incomparable (Theorem~\ref{thm:C}
and Corollary~\ref{cor:E})}

\begin{mainthm}[Incomparability]
\label{thm:C}
Fix $M \geq 1$ and positive sampling levels $0 < d_1 < \cdots < d_M$.
For every $K \geq 1$,
\[
  \Ofinite^{(K)} \incomp \Otheta \qquad \text{on } \Hsym.
\]
\end{mainthm}

\begin{remark}[Scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:C} is a statement about two observation maps and the
refinement preorder (Definition~\ref{def:preorder}); it does not directly involve the predicate $P$.
Incomparability means each map can distinguish some pair the other
cannot---not that either alone obstructs a proof strategy for $P$.
See Remark~\ref{rem:theta-not-P} for $P$-separation by $\Otheta$
(Definition~\ref{def:Otheta}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Witness 1: \texorpdfstring{$\Ofinite^{(K)} \npreceq \Otheta$}{O-theta does not refine O-fin}}

For $T > 0$ and $\sigma_0 \in (1/2, 1)$, the multiset $Q(\sigma_0, T)$
has all imaginary parts equal to $\pm T$, so $\Otheta(Q(\sigma_0, T))$
depends only on $T$, not on $\sigma_0$.

Taking $T = 10$: $\Otheta(Q(3/4, 10)) = \Otheta(Q(9/10, 10))$ at
every level $d_m$.  However:
\begin{align*}
  \Li_1(Q(3/4,10)) &= \frac{102592}{2576009} \approx 0.039826,\\
  \Li_1(Q(9/10,10)) &= \frac{4003600}{100820081} \approx 0.039710.
\end{align*}
Exact difference: $\frac{30024117552}{259713436036729} > 0$
(verified by exact rational arithmetic).
Hence $\Ofinite^{(K)} \npreceq \Otheta$.

\subsection{The non-cancellation lemma}

% Original existence lemma; constructive proof via continuity and Vandermonde non-singularity
\begin{lemma}[Non-cancellation parameter selection]
\label{lem:noncancel}
Fix $K \geq 1$ and $d_* > 0$.  There exist rational parameters
$0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_K < T < d_*$ such that the vector
$\beta = -C^{-1}q(T) \in \QQ^K$ satisfies
\[
  G_K := 2 + \sum_{k=1}^K \beta_k \neq 0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The entries of $C$, $q(T)$, and $\beta = -C^{-1}q(T)$ are rational
functions of $(t_1,\ldots,t_K,T)$ on the open region
$\Omega = \{0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_K < T\}$.
The set $\Omega$ is connected.
Thus $G_K = 2 + \sum_k \beta_k$ is a real-analytic function on $\Omega$.

As $T \to \infty$ with $t_k$ fixed, $q_j(T) = O(T^{-2}) \to 0$,
so $\beta \to 0$ and $G_K \to 2 \neq 0$.  Hence $G_K$ is not identically
zero on $\Omega$.  By the identity theorem for real-analytic functions,
$G_K \not\equiv 0$ on any connected open subset of $\Omega$, in particular
on $\Omega_{d_*} = \{0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_K < T < d_*\}$.
The set $\{G_K \neq 0\}$ is open and dense in $\Omega_{d_*}$; since
rational points are dense, one can choose all parameters rational with
$G_K \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The lemma is necessary: the exact rational counterexample $K=2$,
$t_1 = 1/24$, $t_2 = 9/40$, $T = 3/8$ gives $G_2 = 0$
(verified by exact computation).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Witness 2: \texorpdfstring{$\Otheta \npreceq \Ofinite^{(K)}$}{O-fin does not refine O-theta}}

\begin{proof}[Proof of $\Otheta \npreceq \Ofinite^{(K)}$]
Fix $K \geq 1$.  Choose rational $0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_K < T < d_1$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:noncancel} with $d_* = d_1$, so $G_K \neq 0$.
Construct the Theorem~\ref{thm:A} collision pair: $R = \mathrm{lcm}(\mathrm{denom}(\beta))$,
$n_k = R\beta_k$, $B = \max_k|n_k|$.  Let $\mathcal{Z}_+$ contain $B$
copies of $L(t_k)$ and $\mathcal{Z}_-$ contain $(B+n_k)$ copies of
$L(t_k)$ plus $R$ copies of $Q(3/4,T)$.
By the construction in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, $\Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}_-)$.

Since all $t_k < T < d_1$ and all atoms of $\mathcal{Z}_\pm$ have
$|\im\rho| \in \{t_k\} \cup \{T\}$ with $t_k < T < d_1$, we compute:
\[
  N_{\mathcal{Z}_-}(d_1) - N_{\mathcal{Z}_+}(d_1)
  = 2\sum_{k=1}^K n_k + 4R
  = 2R\!\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^K \beta_k + 2\Bigr)
  = 2R \, G_K \neq 0.
\]
Hence $\Otheta(\mathcal{Z}_-) \neq \Otheta(\mathcal{Z}_+)$,
proving $\Otheta \npreceq \Ofinite^{(K)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
Both directions are established:
$\Ofinite^{(K)} \npreceq \Otheta$ by Witness~1
and $\Otheta \npreceq \Ofinite^{(K)}$ by Witness~2.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Predicate non-determination by \texorpdfstring{$\Otheta$}{O-theta}}

\begin{remark}[$\Otheta$ does not determine $P$]
\label{rem:theta-not-P}
Fix any $T > d_M$ and $\sigma_0 \in (0,1)\setminus\{1/2\}$.  Take
$\mathcal{Z}_+ = 2L(T)$ (two copies of $L(T)$, giving four atoms on
the critical line) and $\mathcal{Z}_- = Q(\sigma_0, T)$ (four atoms off
the critical line).  Both have all imaginary parts equal to $\pm T$, so
since $T > d_M \geq d_m$ for every sampling level, no atom satisfies
$|\im\rho| \leq d_m$; hence
$\Otheta(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Otheta(\mathcal{Z}_-) = (0,\ldots,0)$.
But $P(\mathcal{Z}_+)=1$ and $P(\mathcal{Z}_-)=0$.
Hence $\Otheta$ alone does not determine $P$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Joint observation also admits an exact obstruction}

\begin{maincor}[Joint obstruction]
\label{cor:E}
Fix $M \geq 1$, sampling levels $0 < d_1 < \cdots < d_M$, and $K \geq 1$.
The combined observation $\Ofinite^{(K)}\times\Otheta$ admits an exact
information obstruction: there exist $\mathcal{Z}_+, \mathcal{Z}_- \in \Hsym$
with $P(\mathcal{Z}_+)=1$, $P(\mathcal{Z}_-)=0$, and
$(\Ofinite^{(K)}\times\Otheta)(\mathcal{Z}_+) =
 (\Ofinite^{(K)}\times\Otheta)(\mathcal{Z}_-)$.
\end{maincor}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with $m = K$ and parameters $d_M < t_1 < \cdots < t_K < T$
(all atom heights strictly above $d_M$, as permitted by the $H > 0$ clause
in Theorem~\ref{thm:A}).  Then all atoms of both $\mathcal{Z}_\pm$
have $|\im\rho| > d_M \geq d_m$ for every sampling level, so
$N_{\mathcal{Z}_\pm}(d_m) = 0$ and $\Otheta(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Otheta(\mathcal{Z}_-)
= (0,\ldots,0)$.  By the construction in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, $\Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}_+) =
\Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ and $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) \neq P(\mathcal{Z}_-)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Corollary~\ref{cor:E} supersedes the earlier ``open problem'' of whether the joint
observation $\Ofinite^{(K)}\times\Otheta$ admits an obstruction:
it does, as a direct consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with parameter choice
above all sampling levels.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{What the results say together}

\begin{itemize}
\item Theorems~A and~B together: \emph{no fixed finite initial segment of
  standard Li values, however large, can determine $P$ on $\Hsym$.}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} achieves exact non-determination; Theorem~\ref{thm:B} says the gap
  cannot be bounded away from zero.  In particular, Corollary~\ref{cor:B1Li} shows
  that positive Li values are not sufficient to enforce $P=1$: any $P=1$ witness
  with $\Li_j > 0$ admits a $P=0$ companion with the same sign pattern.

\item Theorem~\ref{thm:C}: \emph{Li values and counting data are not
  redundant---each can distinguish inputs the other cannot.}
  Corollary~\ref{cor:E} then shows the joint observation is still obstructed
  (via the Theorem~\ref{thm:A} construction with parameters above all sampling levels).

\item Corollary~\ref{cor:D}: \emph{finite Euler-product information leaves a gap
  within the Helson class.}  The obstruction is existential (Andersson's
  theorem does not provide explicit characters).
\end{itemize}

The principal limitation is that $\Hsym$ is a class of formal finite
multisets; the actual zero set of $\zeta$ is infinite and subject to
the Hadamard product, functional equation, Euler product, and
Riemann--von Mangoldt counting law --- none of which are imposed on $\Hsym$.
Theorems~A--C establish non-identifiability of \emph{finite statistics}
on formal symmetric multisets; they do not constitute barriers for the
full analytic structure of $\zeta$.

\subsection{Relation to Bombieri--Lagarias}

Bombieri--Lagarias~\cite{BombieriLagarias1999} study the Li criterion for
arbitrary (possibly complex and infinite) multisets, proving that the
criterion applied to the multiset of $\zeta$-zeros is equivalent to RH.  The present paper restricts to the symmetric finite class $\Hsym$
and shows that \emph{truncations} $(\Li_1,\ldots,\Li_m)$ are
non-identifying within this class.  The key addition is the explicit
rational-arithmetic construction (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}), which yields exact collisions
rather than approximate ones, and the exact quantifier structure of the
obstruction.

\subsection{Escape routes}

The following methods are \emph{not} blocked by the results in this paper:

\begin{enumerate}
\item An unbounded hierarchy of Li tests ($m \to \infty$, simultaneously).
\item The full Euler product $\prod_p (1-p^{-s})^{-1}$ plus functional equation.
\item Selberg-class axioms (for Corollary~\ref{cor:D} only).
\item Methods using the imaginary part of $\log\zeta(1/2+it)$ continuously.
\end{enumerate}

Theorems~A--C also do not address: uniform height upper bounds on
atom positions, fixed total atom count, the Riemann--von Mangoldt
counting asymptotics, or Hadamard/functional-equation/Euler-product constraints.

\subsection{Open problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Grow-with-$T$ obstruction.}  Is there an exact
  information obstruction for $\Ofinite^{(K(T))}$ that remains valid even
  as $K = K(T)$ grows with the height parameter $T$ while satisfying a
  uniform bound $\#\mathcal{Z}_\pm \leq T^A$ on total atom count or denominator height?

\item \textbf{Weil-type observations.}
  For a fixed finite family $h_1, \ldots, h_r \in C_c^\infty(\RR)$ of
  real-valued, even functions, the observations
  $\varphi_{h_i}(\rho) = \hat{h}_i((\rho-\tfrac12)/i)$ are entire functions
  of $\rho$ with real values on the critical line.
  The Chebyshev--Vandermonde reduction used in Theorem~\ref{thm:A}
  does not directly apply because the resulting observation values are
  not guaranteed to be rational, and no general transcendence statement
  is available.  Does an analogue of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} hold
  for such observations?

\item \textbf{Information hierarchy convergence.}
  Is there a natural sequence of observations
  $O_1 \preceq O_2 \preceq \cdots$ converging to $O_{\mathrm{oracle}}$
  (the identity observation $O_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\mathcal{Z})=\mathcal{Z}$)
  in some appropriate sense, and at what level does the chain separate
  $P = 1$ from $P = 0$?
\end{enumerate}
