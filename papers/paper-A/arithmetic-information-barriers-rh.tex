\title{Arithmetic Information Barriers for the Riemann Hypothesis}

\author{Lin Tao}
\date{2026}

\begin{abstract}
We prove three theorems showing that natural classes of finite-arithmetic
proof strategies for the Riemann Hypothesis face provable information
obstructions. The ambient class is a collection of finite zero multisets
symmetric under complex conjugation and the functional-equation reflection
$\rho \mapsto 1-\rho$, equipped with an information-refinement preorder.
\textbf{Theorem~B2} (exact Li collision): for every fixed finite collection
of Li-type test functions, there exist two multisets that are indistinguishable
by those tests yet differ in whether all atoms lie on the critical line.
\textbf{Theorem~B1} (no uniform separation): no fixed finite Li test family
enforces a strictly positive gap between on-line and off-line multisets.
\textbf{Theorem~H$'$(i)} (incomparability): the Li observation map and the
sampled counting map are incomparable in the refinement preorder — each can
distinguish objects the other cannot. We also record, as a corollary of
Andersson~\cite{Andersson2024}, that no finite Euler-factor constraint on a
Helson zeta function forces its zeros to lie on the critical line.

None of these results assume or imply the Riemann Hypothesis.
They are barrier theorems in the sense of Razborov--Rudich~\cite{RazborovRudich1997}:
they establish limits on natural \emph{proof methods}, not on the truth of RH.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------

Li's criterion~\cite{Li1997} asserts that RH is equivalent to the nonnegativity
of all Li coefficients $\lambda_n = \sum_\rho [1-(1-1/\rho)^n]$, where the sum
runs over nontrivial zeros of~$\zeta$.  This reformulation sparked a research
programme: can one establish RH by proving, for each~$n$ individually or for
long finite stretches, that $\lambda_n \geq 0$?  The results of this paper say
that any such strategy faces a fundamental obstruction: finitely many Li
inequalities carry insufficient information to determine whether all zeros lie on
the critical line.

\subsection*{What is an information barrier?}

We use ``information barrier'' in a precise technical sense, following the
programme set out in~\cite{ProgramV2}.  Fix a class $\mathcal{M}$ of admissible
proof methods and an \emph{observation map} $O : \Xsym \to S$, where $\Xsym$
is the ambient class of admissible zero multisets and $S$ is a set of
observable records.  An \emph{information obstruction} for the pair
$(\mathcal{M}, O)$ is an explicit pair $(\mathcal{Z}_+, \mathcal{Z}_-)$ such
that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $O(\mathcal{Z}_+) = O(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ (the method cannot distinguish the two inputs);
  \item the target predicate $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) \neq P(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ (the two
  inputs give opposite answers to the question the method is trying to decide);
  \item neither $\mathcal{Z}_+$ nor $\mathcal{Z}_-$ involves RH as a hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}

This is the zero-multiset analogue of a Razborov--Rudich natural proof barrier:
it shows that a method constrained to observe only $O(\mathcal{Z})$ cannot, in
principle, prove $P(\mathcal{Z}) = 1$ for all admissible inputs.  An information
barrier does not prevent RH from being true, nor does it prevent an RH proof
using different methods.  The escape route (the set of methods \emph{not}
blocked) is named explicitly in each theorem.

\subsection*{Results}

The ambient class $\Hsym$ consists of finite zero multisets in the open critical
strip $\{0 < \re \rho < 1\}$, closed under $\rho \mapsto \bar\rho$ and
$\rho \mapsto 1 - \rho$ (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).  The target predicate is
$P(\mathcal{Z}) = 1$ iff every atom has $\re \rho = 1/2$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Theorem~B2} (Section~\ref{sec:B2}): For every fixed finite
  Li-type test family $\Phi = (\varphi_1, \ldots, \varphi_m)$, there exist
  $\mathcal{Z}_+, \mathcal{Z}_- \in \Hsym$ with $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 1$,
  $P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$, and $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-)$
  exactly for $j = 1, \ldots, m$.  The collision is realised by explicit
  rational-arithmetic witnesses.

\item \textbf{Theorem~B1} (Section~\ref{sec:B1}): No fixed finite Li test
  family admits a uniform strictly positive lower bound separating
  $P(\mathcal{Z}) = 1$ from $P(\mathcal{Z}) = 0$ within $\Hsym$.

\item \textbf{Theorem~H$'$(i)} (Section~\ref{sec:H}): Fix $M \geq 1$ and
  positive sampling levels $0 < d_1 < \cdots < d_M$.  For every $K \geq 1$,
  the Li observation map $\Ofinite^{(K)}$ and the sampled counting map $\Otheta$ are
  incomparable in the information refinement preorder on $\Hsym$.

\item \textbf{Corollary~C} (Section~\ref{sec:C}): For every finite prime cutoff
  $P_0$ and every $z_1$ with $0 < \re z_1 < 1$, $\re z_1 \neq 1/2$, there
  exists a Helson zeta function that agrees with $\zeta$ on all Euler factors
  $p \leq P_0$ yet has a zero at $z_1$.
\end{itemize}

All four results are unconditional; none assumes RH.

\subsection*{Comparison with prior work}

\textbf{Li's criterion and reformulations.}
Li's criterion~\cite{Li1997} asserts RH is equivalent to $\lambda_n \geq 0$
for all $n$; Bombieri--Lagarias~\cite{BombieriLagarias1999} gave an equivalent
formulation in terms of explicit-formula positivity.  These are RH-\emph{equivalences},
not barriers: they locate difficulty but do not block proof methods.
The distinction is categorical: an equivalence $C \Leftrightarrow \mathrm{RH}$
says the problem is hard; our results say a \emph{specific approach to that problem}
cannot work.

\textbf{Weil explicit formula and positivity.}
The Weil explicit formula expresses sums over zeros as sums over primes plus
archimedean contributions; positivity of these sums for all test functions would
imply RH.  Connes~\cite{ConnesWeilFF1999} interpreted this as a spectral
absorption problem on an adelic space, and Bombieri~\cite{Bombieri2000} reformulated
it as a positivity problem on $L^2$ spaces over the primes.  These approaches
use \emph{all} test functions simultaneously; our results apply precisely when only
a \emph{finite} family is used.

\textbf{Selberg trace formula and spectral methods.}
The Selberg trace formula connects eigenvalues of the Laplacian on hyperbolic
surfaces to geodesic lengths, and analogies with the Riemann zeta function
have been studied extensively (see Hejhal~\cite{Hejhal1976}).
Connes--Consani--Moscovici~\cite{CCM2025} construct zeta spectral triples
whose Dirac-operator regularized determinant encodes $\hat{\xi}(z)$; the
open step is a normalization condition on those determinants.

\textbf{Helson class and Dirichlet series.}
Helson~\cite{Helson1969} introduced random Dirichlet series with unimodular
coefficients; their zero distribution was studied by Seip~\cite{Seip2020} and
Bochkov--Romanov~\cite{BochkovRomanov2022}.  Andersson~\cite{Andersson2024}
proved that prescribed zeros in the critical strip can be realized by Helson
zeta functions; Corollary~C of this paper makes the finite-Euler-factor
limitation explicit as an information obstruction.

\textbf{Complexity-theoretic analogues.}
The closest formal analogues of our results in complexity theory are the
Razborov--Rudich natural proofs barrier~\cite{RazborovRudich1997}
(any ``natural'' proof technique for circuit lower bounds actually diagonalizes
against itself) and the relativization barrier~\cite{BakerGillSolovay1975}.
Our information-obstruction framework is a direct adaptation of the
Razborov--Rudich structure: we identify a class of proof methods
(those using only $\Ofinite^{(K)}$ or only $\Otheta$), construct explicit
adversarial witnesses, and conclude that the class cannot decide the target
predicate.

Theorem~C is a corollary of Andersson~\cite{Andersson2024}; we record it here
for completeness and to make the Euler-factor limitation explicit in the
framework.

\section{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Ambient class}

\begin{definition}[Ambient class $\Hsym$]
\label{def:ambient}
A finite multiset $\mathcal{Z}$ of complex numbers is in $\Hsym$ if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(S\arabic*)}]
  \item\label{itm:strip} every atom satisfies $0 < \re\rho < 1$;
  \item\label{itm:conj} $\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$ implies $\bar\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$
    with the same multiplicity;
  \item\label{itm:fe} $\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$ implies $1-\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$
    with the same multiplicity.
\end{enumerate}
The target predicate is $P(\mathcal{Z}) = 1$ iff $\re\rho = 1/2$ for every
$\rho \in \mathcal{Z}$.
\end{definition}

Condition~\ref{itm:strip} excludes $0$ and $1$, ensuring that $\varphi_j(\rho)$
and $\varphi_j(1-\rho)$ are well-defined for all Li-type tests.
Conditions~\ref{itm:conj} and~\ref{itm:fe} mimic the two symmetries of the
nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$.

\begin{remark}[Finiteness]
We work with \emph{finite} multisets throughout.  This is the natural domain for
the finite-observation theorems (B2, B1, H).  Corollary~C involves an infinite
Helson zeta function, whose zero multiset in the critical strip is treated
separately.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Li-type tests and the finite observation map}

Fix $m \geq 1$ and \emph{Li-type test functions}
\[
  \varphi_j(\rho) = 1 - \bigl(1 - \rho^{-1}\bigr)^j, \qquad j = 1, \ldots, m.
\]
More generally, one may take $\varphi_j$ to be moment-type ($\varphi_j(\rho) = \rho^{-k_j}$)
or Weil-type (real-valued even $h \in C_c^\infty(\RR)$ with $\varphi_j(\rho)=\hat h(\im\rho)$), provided each $\varphi_j$ is
meromorphic in $\{0 < \re\rho < 1\}$ and satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(W\arabic*)}]
  \item\label{itm:realval} $\varphi_j(\bar\rho) = \overline{\varphi_j(\rho)}$
    (ensures real-valued observations), and
  \item\label{itm:decay} $|\varphi_j(\sigma + it)| \leq C_j(1 + |t|)^{-1-\delta_j}$
    uniformly in $\sigma \in (0,1)$ for some $C_j, \delta_j > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
The standard Li tests satisfy \ref{itm:decay} with $\delta_j = 1$.

\begin{definition}[Li observation and finite observation map]
\label{def:Li}
For $\mathcal{Z} \in \Hsym$ and $j \geq 1$, set
\[
  \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}) = 2 \sum_{\rho \in \mathcal{Z}} \varphi_j(\rho).
\]
This sum is real by \ref{itm:realval} and the reflection symmetry
\ref{itm:fe}: since $\mathcal{Z}$ is closed under $\rho \mapsto 1 - \rho$,
we have $\sum_\rho \varphi_j(\rho) = \sum_\rho \varphi_j(1-\rho)$, and
condition \ref{itm:realval} plus conjugation symmetry~\ref{itm:conj} then
forces the sum to be real.

The \emph{finite observation map} with $K$ tests is
\[
  \Ofinite^{(K)} : \Hsym \longrightarrow \RR^K, \qquad
  \Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}) = \bigl(\Li_1(\mathcal{Z}), \ldots, \Li_K(\mathcal{Z})\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Normalization convention]
\label{rem:normconv}
Two conventions appear in the literature.  The \emph{R-symm} convention
(used here) defines $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}) = 2\sum_{\rho \in \mathcal{Z}} \varphi_j(\rho)$;
the \emph{R-atom} convention sums $\varphi_j$ once per atom without the factor~$2$.
On a multiset closed under $\rho \mapsto 1-\rho$ these differ by exactly a factor~$2$,
and every qualitative conclusion (existence of collisions, absence of uniform gaps)
is convention-independent.  All numerical anchors in this paper use R-symm.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sampled counting map}

\begin{definition}[Sampled counting map]
\label{def:Otheta}
Fix $M \geq 1$ and positive sampling levels $0 < d_1 < \cdots < d_M$.  For
$\mathcal{Z} \in \Hsym$, let $N_{\mathcal{Z}}(u) = \#\{\rho \in \mathcal{Z} : |\im\rho| \leq u\}$
(with multiplicity).  The \emph{sampled counting map} is
\[
  \Otheta : \Hsym \longrightarrow \Z_{\geq 0}^M, \qquad
  \Otheta(\mathcal{Z}) = \bigl(N_{\mathcal{Z}}(d_1), \ldots, N_{\mathcal{Z}}(d_M)\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

The restriction to positive levels $d_m > 0$ ensures this map is non-constant:
taking two symmetric quartets with different imaginary parts, one below $d_1$
and one above $d_M$, gives different first coordinates.

\subsection{Information refinement preorder}

\begin{definition}[Refinement preorder]
\label{def:preorder}
For two observation maps $O_a, O_b : \Hsym \to S_a$ and $\Hsym \to S_b$,
write $O_a \preceq O_b$ (``$O_b$ refines $O_a$'') if there is a function
$f$ such that $O_a = f \circ O_b$.  Write $O_a \bowtie O_b$ for
\emph{incomparability}: $O_a \npreceq O_b$ and $O_b \npreceq O_a$.
\end{definition}

This is a preorder (reflexive and transitive) but not in general antisymmetric.
No lattice structure is claimed.

\subsection{Building blocks: the on-line pair and the off-line quartet}

Two basic building blocks recur throughout:
\begin{align*}
  L(t) &= \{{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}} + it,\; {\textstyle\frac{1}{2}} - it\} && \text{(critical-line conjugate pair, }t > 0\text{)},\\
  Q(\sigma_0, T) &= \{\sigma_0 \pm iT,\; 1-\sigma_0 \pm iT\} && \text{(off-line symmetric quartet, }\sigma_0 \neq {\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\text{)}.
\end{align*}
Both are in $\Hsym$ for any $0 < \sigma_0 < 1$ and $t, T > 0$.
We have $P(L(t)) = 1$ and $P(Q(\sigma_0,T)) = 0$ (for $\sigma_0 \neq 1/2$).

Direct computation using~Definition~\ref{def:Li} gives:
\begin{align}
  \Li_j(L(t))         &= 4\,\re\varphi_j\!\bigl({\textstyle\frac12}+it\bigr), \label{eq:Lionline}\\
  \Li_j(Q(\sigma_0,T)) &= 4\,\re\bigl[\varphi_j(\sigma_0+iT) + \varphi_j(1-\sigma_0+iT)\bigr]. \label{eq:Lioffl}
\end{align}
For the standard Li tests, $|\Li_j(Q(\sigma_0,T))| = O(T^{-1})$ as $T \to \infty$.

\section{Li observations admit exact collisions (Theorem B2)}
\label{sec:B2}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Statement}

\begin{theorem}[B2 --- exact Li collision]
\label{thm:B2}
For every $m \geq 1$ and every family of test functions
$\Phi = (\varphi_1, \ldots, \varphi_m)$ satisfying \textup{\ref{itm:realval}}
and \textup{\ref{itm:decay}}, there exist multisets
$\mathcal{Z}_+, \mathcal{Z}_- \in \Hsym$ with $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 1$,
$P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$, and
\[
  \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) \qquad \text{for all } j = 1, \ldots, m.
\]
Moreover, $\mathcal{Z}_\pm$ can be taken to have rational heights and all
multiplicities nonnegative integers.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The collision matrix}

Fix distinct positive rationals $0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_m$ and a rational
$T > t_m$.  Let $\mathcal{Z}_+$ consist of $M$ copies of each $L(t_k)$ (for a
buffer $M \geq 1$ to be chosen), and let $\mathcal{Z}_-$ be obtained from
$\mathcal{Z}_+$ by adding $n_k$ copies of $L(t_k)$ (where $n_k \in \Z$ with
$M + n_k \geq 0$) and $R$ copies of $Q(3/4, T)$.

The exact collision condition $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-)$
becomes, for $j = 1, \ldots, m$:
\[
  \sum_{k=1}^m n_k \, C_{jk} + R \, d_j(T) = 0, \tag{$\star$}\label{eq:collision}
\]
where
\begin{align*}
  C_{jk} &= \Li_j(L(t_k)) = 4\,\re\varphi_j\!\bigl({\textstyle\frac12}+it_k\bigr),\\
  d_j(T) &= \Li_j(Q(3/4,T)) = 4\,\re\bigl[\varphi_j({\textstyle\frac34}+iT) + \varphi_j({\textstyle\frac14}+iT)\bigr].
\end{align*}
In matrix form, $Cn + Rd(T) = 0$ where $C \in \RR^{m \times m}$ and
$d(T) \in \RR^m$.

\subsection{Non-vanishing of the quartet tail}

\begin{lemma}[Positivity of $d_1(T)$]
\label{lem:d1pos}
For every $T > 0$, $d_1(T) > 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the standard Li test $\varphi_1(\rho) = 1 - (1 - \rho^{-1}) = \rho^{-1}$,
one computes
\[
  d_1(T) = 4\,\re\bigl[\tfrac{1}{\frac34+iT} + \tfrac{1}{\frac14+iT}\bigr]
         = 4\biggl(\frac{3/4}{(3/4)^2+T^2} + \frac{1/4}{(1/4)^2+T^2}\biggr) > 0.
\]
The explicit rational formula is
$d_1(T) = \frac{64(16T^2+3)}{(256T^4+160T^2+9)}$,
whose numerator $64(16T^2+3)$ and denominator $256T^4+160T^2+9$ are sums of
positive terms, so $d_1(T) > 0$ for all real~$T$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Non-singularity of the Li collision matrix}

\begin{lemma}[Full rank of $C$]
\label{lem:Crank}
For distinct $t_1, \ldots, t_m > 0$, the matrix $C = (C_{jk})$ with
$C_{jk} = 4(1 - T_j(x_k))$ \textup{(}where $x_k = (4t_k^2 - 1)/(4t_k^2+1)$
and $T_j$ is the degree-$j$ Chebyshev polynomial\textup{)} satisfies $\det C \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We reduce to a Vandermonde determinant.  Since $T_j(1) = 1$, we have
$1 - T_j(x) = (1-x)\,q_j(x)$ for a polynomial $q_j$ of degree exactly $j-1$
with leading coefficient $2^{j-1}$.  Thus
\[
  C_{jk} = 4(1-x_k)\,q_j(x_k),
\]
and $C = 4 \cdot [q_j(x_k)] \cdot \operatorname{diag}(1-x_k)$.
Since $x_k \in (-1,1)$ each factor $1-x_k > 0$, so $\det C \neq 0$ iff
$\det[q_j(x_k)] \neq 0$.

The polynomials $\{q_j\}_{j=1}^m$ have degrees $0, 1, \ldots, m-1$ with
positive leading coefficients; they form a lower-triangular basis over the
monomial basis.  Writing $q_j(x) = \sum_{i=1}^j a_{ji} x^{i-1}$ with
$a_{jj} = 2^{j-1} > 0$, the evaluation matrix factors as
$[q_j(x_k)] = A \cdot V(x_1, \ldots, x_m)$
where $A$ is lower-triangular with diagonal $2^{j-1} > 0$
and $V(x_1,\ldots,x_m)$ is the Vandermonde matrix with $(i,k)$-entry $x_k^{i-1}$.
Since the $t_k$ are distinct and $x(t) = (4t^2-1)/(4t^2+1)$ is strictly
increasing, the $x_k$ are distinct, so $\det V \neq 0$.  Hence $\det C \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The same rank argument applies to moment-type tests $\varphi_j(\rho) = \rho^{-j}$
via a cosine-Vandermonde reduction on $\cos\theta_k$ where $\theta_k = 2\arctan(2t_k)$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Rational solution and non-negative integer multiplicities}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B2}]
Choose distinct rational $t_1 < \cdots < t_m$ and rational $T > t_m$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:Crank} gives $C$ invertible over $\Q$ (the matrix entries are
rational when $t_k, T$ are rational).  Set
\[
  \beta = -C^{-1} d(T) \in \Q^m.
\]
Let $R = \operatorname{lcm}(\text{denominators of }\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m) \in \Z_{\geq 1}$ and
$n_k = R\beta_k \in \Z$.  Then $Cn + Rd(T) = 0$, i.e.~\eqref{eq:collision} holds.

\textbf{Non-negative multiplicities.} Set $M = \max_k |n_k|$.  Then
$M + n_k \geq 0$ for all~$k$.  Define:
\[
  \mathcal{Z}_+ = \bigsqcup_{k=1}^m (M+n_k) \cdot L(t_k) \cdot \{n_k \geq 0\} \cup M \cdot L(t_k) \cdot \{n_k < 0\},
\]
i.e., $\mathcal{Z}_+$ contains $M$ copies of each $L(t_k)$ (if $n_k < 0$,
these will be ``removed'' to form $\mathcal{Z}_-$; if $n_k \geq 0$, one can
take $\mathcal{Z}_+$ to contain $(M + n_k)$ and $\mathcal{Z}_-$ to contain $M$
copies, or equivalently start from $M$ copies and add $n_k \geq 0$ copies to
form $\mathcal{Z}_-$).

More precisely: let $\mathcal{Z}_+$ contain $M$ copies of $L(t_k)$ for each $k$,
and let $\mathcal{Z}_-$ contain $(M + n_k)$ copies of $L(t_k)$ plus $R$ copies
of $Q(3/4, T)$.  Both are in $\Hsym$; all multiplicities are $\geq 0$ since
$M + n_k \geq 0$.

\textbf{Observation collision.} By construction $Cn + Rd(T) = 0$, which is
exactly $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) - \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 0$ for each $j$.

\textbf{Predicate separation.}  $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 1$ (all atoms on $\re\rho = 1/2$).
$P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$ since $\mathcal{Z}_-$ contains atoms at $\re\rho = 3/4$
from the $R \geq 1$ copies of $Q(3/4,T)$; $R \geq 1$ follows from
$d(T) \neq 0$ (which holds because $d_1(T) > 0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:d1pos}, so
$d(T) \neq 0$, hence $\beta = -C^{-1}d(T) \neq 0$, hence $R \geq 1$).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Numerical illustration ($m=2$, exact rational arithmetic)}

We exhibit the complete collision for $m=2$ with parameters
$t_1=1$, $t_2=2$, $T=3$, $\sigma_0=\frac{3}{4}$.

\textbf{Chebyshev nodes.}
$x_k = (4t_k^2-1)/(4t_k^2+1)$:
\[
  x_1 = \tfrac{3}{5}, \qquad x_2 = \tfrac{15}{17}.
\]

\textbf{Collision matrix.}  $C_{jk} = 4(1-T_j(x_k))$, where
$T_1(x)=x$, $T_2(x)=2x^2-1$:
\[
  C = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{8}{5} & \tfrac{8}{17} \\[4pt]
                      \tfrac{128}{25} & \tfrac{512}{289} \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \det C = \tfrac{3072}{7225} \neq 0.
\]

\textbf{Quartet tail.}  $d_j = \Li_j(Q(3/4, 3))$ via \eqref{eq:Lioffl}:
\[
  d_1 = \tfrac{3136}{7395}, \qquad d_2 = \tfrac{10041088}{6076225}.
\]

\textbf{Rational solution.}  $\beta = -C^{-1}d$:
\[
  \beta_1 = \tfrac{7978}{128673}, \qquad \beta_2 = -\tfrac{42082}{37845}.
\]
Clearing denominators: $R = \mathrm{lcm}(128673,\, 37845) = 643365$, giving
\[
  n_1 = R\beta_1 = 39890, \qquad n_2 = R\beta_2 = -715394.
\]
Buffer: $M = 715394$, so $M+n_1 = 755284 \geq 0$ and $M+n_2 = 0 \geq 0$.

\textbf{Exact zero residual} (verified by Python \texttt{fractions.Fraction}):
\[
  C_{j1}n_1 + C_{j2}n_2 + R\,d_j = 0 \quad \text{for } j=1,2.
\]

\textbf{The collision pair.}  $\mathcal{Z}_+$ contains $M = 715394$ copies of
$L(1)$ and $L(2)$; $\mathcal{Z}_-$ contains $755284$ copies of $L(1)$,
$0$ copies of $L(2)$, and $R = 643365$ copies of $Q(3/4, 3)$.
Both are in $\Hsym$ with nonneg integer multiplicities;
$\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ exactly for $j=1,2$;
and $P(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 1$, $P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$.

\section{No uniform Li separation (Theorem B1)}
\label{sec:B1}

\subsection{Statement}

\begin{theorem}[B1 --- no uniform separation]
\label{thm:B1}
Fix $m \geq 1$ and a test family $\Phi$ satisfying \textup{\ref{itm:realval}}
and \textup{\ref{itm:decay}}.  Let $\mathcal{Z}_+ \in \Hsym$ satisfy
$P(\mathcal{Z}_+) = 1$ and suppose each $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+)$ strictly exceeds
a threshold $c_j \in \RR$.  Then for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists
$\mathcal{Z}_- \in \Hsym$ with $P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$ and
\[
  |\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) - \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+)| < \varepsilon \qquad
  \text{for all } j = 1, \ldots, m.
\]
In particular, $\mathcal{Z}_-$ also satisfies $\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_-) > c_j$
whenever $\varepsilon < \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) - c_j$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Add a single off-line quartet $Q(3/4, T)$ to $\mathcal{Z}_+$ to obtain
$\mathcal{Z}'_- = \mathcal{Z}_+ \sqcup Q(3/4, T)$.  Then
$\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}'_-) = \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+) + d_j(T)$, and $d_j(T) \to 0$
as $T \to \infty$ by condition~\ref{itm:decay}.  Hence for large enough $T$,
$|\Li_j(\mathcal{Z}'_-) - \Li_j(\mathcal{Z}_+)| < \varepsilon$ for all~$j$.
Since $Q(3/4,T)$ contributes atoms at $\re\rho = 3/4 \neq 1/2$, we have
$P(\mathcal{Z}'_-) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[B1-Li]
\label{cor:B1Li}
For every fixed $K$, satisfying the first $K$ strict positivity conditions
$\lambda_j > 0$ for $j = 1, \ldots, K$ does not determine whether all atoms
lie on the critical line: there exist $\mathcal{Z}_- \in \Hsym$ with
$P(\mathcal{Z}_-) = 0$ satisfying all $K$ conditions.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Relation to Theorem B2}

Theorem~B1 gives approximate non-discrimination (tolerance $\varepsilon$);
Theorem~B2 gives exact non-discrimination ($\varepsilon = 0$).  B1 requires
only the decay condition~\ref{itm:decay}, while B2 additionally requires
the matrix $C$ to be invertible over $\Q$.  Both escape routes are identical:
a method using an infinite hierarchy $m \to \infty$ of tests, an Euler product
constraint, or the functional equation is not blocked by either theorem.

%%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Li and counting observations are incomparable (Theorem H$'$(i))}
\label{sec:H}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Statement}

\begin{theorem}[H$'$(i) --- incomparability]
\label{thm:Hincomp}
Fix $M \geq 1$ and positive sampling levels $0 < d_1 < \cdots < d_M$.  For
every $K \geq 1$,
\[
  \Ofinite^{(K)} \bowtie \Otheta \qquad \text{on } \Hsym.
\]
That is, $\Ofinite^{(K)} \npreceq \Otheta$ and $\Otheta \npreceq \Ofinite^{(K)}$.
\end{theorem}

Incomparability is the formal expression of the claim that Li observations and
counting observations \emph{capture orthogonal aspects} of the zero multiset:
each can distinguish configurations the other cannot.

\subsection{Witness 1: same-height quartets ($\Ofinite^{(K)} \npreceq \Otheta$)}

For $T > 0$ and $\sigma_0 \in (1/2, 1)$, the multiset $Q(\sigma_0, T)$ has
imaginary parts $\pm T$ (each with multiplicity~$2$), so
$N_{Q(\sigma_0,T)}(u) = 4$ for $u \geq T$ and $= 0$ for $u < T$.
In particular, $\Otheta(Q(\sigma_0, T))$ depends only on~$T$, not on $\sigma_0$.

Taking $T = 10$, $\sigma_0 = 3/4$ and $\sigma_0' = 9/10$:
$\Otheta(Q(3/4, 10)) = \Otheta(Q(9/10, 10))$ at every level $d_m$.

However, using the Li test $\varphi_1(\rho) = \rho^{-1}$ and the normalization
of Definition~\ref{def:Li}:
\begin{align*}
  \Li_1(Q(3/4,10)) &= 4\,\re\bigl[\tfrac{1}{3/4+10i} + \tfrac{1}{1/4+10i}\bigr]
                    = \frac{102592}{2576009} \approx 0.039826,\\
  \Li_1(Q(9/10,10)) &= 4\,\re\bigl[\tfrac{1}{9/10+10i} + \tfrac{1}{1/10+10i}\bigr]
                     = \frac{4003600}{100820081} \approx 0.039710.
\end{align*}
The exact difference is $\frac{30024117552}{259713436036729} > 0$
(verified by exact rational arithmetic).

Therefore the pair $(Q(3/4,10),\, Q(9/10,10))$ satisfies:
$\Otheta$ cannot distinguish them, but $\Ofinite^{(K)}$ can.
This proves $\Ofinite^{(K)} \npreceq \Otheta$.

\subsection{The non-cancellation lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Non-cancellation parameter selection]
\label{lem:noncancel}
Fix $K \geq 1$ and $d_* > 0$.  One can choose rational parameters
$0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_K < T < d_*$ such that the B2 vector
$\beta = -C^{-1}d(T) \in \Q^K$ satisfies
\[
  G_K(t_1, \ldots, t_K, T) := 2 + \sum_{k=1}^K \beta_k \neq 0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed rational $t_1 < \cdots < t_K$, the entries of $C$, $d(T)$, and
$\beta = -C^{-1}d(T)$ are rational functions of $T$.  As $T \to \infty$ with
$t_k$ fixed, $\varphi_j(\sigma + iT) \to 0$ for any $\sigma \in (0,1)$,
so $d(T) \to 0$ and $\beta \to 0$, hence $G_K \to 2 \neq 0$.

Therefore $G_K$ is not the identically zero rational function of $(t_1,\ldots,t_K,T)$.
Its non-vanishing is an open condition; the set where $G_K \neq 0$ is a dense open
subset of the parameter domain $\{0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_K < T < d_*\}$.
Since rational points are dense in this open set, one can choose all parameters
rational with $G_K \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The lemma is necessary: the exact rational counterexample $K=2$, $t_1 = 1/24$,
$t_2 = 9/40$, $T = 3/8$ gives $G_2 = 0$ (verified by exact computation).
The claim ``any B2 pair automatically separates the counting observation'' is
false; one must select parameters avoiding the cancellation locus.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Witness 2: B2 pair with non-cancellation ($\Otheta \npreceq \Ofinite^{(K)}$)}

\begin{proof}[Proof of $\Otheta \npreceq \Ofinite^{(K)}$]
Fix $K \geq 1$.  Choose rational $0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_K < T < d_1$ using
Lemma~\ref{lem:noncancel}, so that the B2 parameters satisfy $G_K \neq 0$.

Construct the B2 collision pair as in Theorem~\ref{thm:B2}: let $R = \operatorname{lcm}(\text{denom of }\beta)$,
$n_k = R\beta_k$, $M = \max_k |n_k|$.  Set $\mathcal{Z}_+$ with $M$ copies of $L(t_k)$
and $\mathcal{Z}_-$ with $(M+n_k)$ copies of $L(t_k)$ plus $R$ copies of $Q(3/4,T)$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:B2}, $\Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}_-)$.

For the counting observation, since $T < d_1$, all atoms of both $\mathcal{Z}_+$
and $\mathcal{Z}_-$ have $|\im\rho| \leq T < d_1$.  Computing:
\begin{align*}
  N_{\mathcal{Z}_-}(d_1) - N_{\mathcal{Z}_+}(d_1)
  &= 2\sum_{k=1}^K n_k + 4R
   = 2R\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^K \beta_k + 2\right)
   = 2R \, G_K.
\end{align*}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:noncancel}, $G_K \neq 0$, and $R \geq 1$, so
$N_{\mathcal{Z}_-}(d_1) \neq N_{\mathcal{Z}_+}(d_1)$.  Hence
$\Otheta(\mathcal{Z}_-) \neq \Otheta(\mathcal{Z}_+)$, proving
$\Otheta \npreceq \Ofinite^{(K)}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:Hincomp}}

Both directions are now established:
$\Ofinite^{(K)} \npreceq \Otheta$ by Witness~1 (same-height quartets, Section~4.2),
and $\Otheta \npreceq \Ofinite^{(K)}$ by Witness~2 (B2 pair with non-cancellation,
Section~4.4).  This gives $\Ofinite^{(K)} \bowtie \Otheta$ for every $K \geq 1$.
\qed

\subsection{Remark on the oracle}

The finest observation is the full multiset $O_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\mathcal{Z}) = \mathcal{Z}$.
Both $\Ofinite^{(K)}$ and $\Otheta$ are strictly coarser: the B2 pair exhibits
$\Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}_+) = \Ofinite^{(K)}(\mathcal{Z}_-)$ with $\mathcal{Z}_+ \neq \mathcal{Z}_-$,
and similarly for $\Otheta$ via the same-height quartet.
A successful RH proof would need to use information strictly beyond either of
these two observation classes --- or a combination that is not blocked.

%%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Finite Euler factors do not force critical-line zeros (Corollary C)}
\label{sec:C}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Setting: Helson zeta functions}

For a completely multiplicative unimodular function $\chi : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{T}$
($|\chi(p)| = 1$ for all primes $p$), define the Helson zeta function
$\zeta_\chi(s) = \sum_{n \geq 1} \chi(n) n^{-s} = \prod_p (1-\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1}$,
convergent for $\re s > 1$.  For a finite prime cutoff $P_0$, say $\chi$ is
\emph{$P_0$-standard} if $\chi(p) = 1$ for all primes $p \leq P_0$.

Let $\mathcal{H}_S$ denote the Helson zeta functions admitting meromorphic
continuation to the open strip $S = \{0 < \re s < 1\}$.  Define the target
predicate $P_S(\zeta_\chi) = 1$ iff every zero $\rho \in S$ of $\zeta_\chi$
satisfies $\re\rho = 1/2$.

\subsection{Statement}

\begin{corollary}[C --- finite Euler factors do not force critical-line zeros]
\label{cor:C}
For every finite prime cutoff $P_0$ and every $z_1 \in S$ with $\re z_1 \neq 1/2$,
there exists a completely multiplicative unimodular $\tilde\chi$ with
$\tilde\chi(p) = 1$ for all $p \leq P_0$ \textup{(}$P_0$-standard\textup{)},
such that $\zeta_{\tilde\chi} \in \mathcal{H}_S$ and $P_S(\zeta_{\tilde\chi}) = 0$.

In particular, for every satisfiable observation value
$a = (a_p)_{p \leq P_0} \in \mathbb{T}^{\pi(P_0)}$, there exists $\zeta \in \mathcal{H}_S$
with the prescribed Euler data and $P_S(\zeta) = 0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Step 1.} By Andersson~\cite[Theorem~5]{Andersson2024}, there exists
a Helson zeta function $\zeta_\chi$ with a simple zero at $z_1 \in S$,
$\re z_1 \neq 1/2$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Define the finite-factor ratio
\[
  R(s) = \prod_{p \leq P_0} \frac{1 - \chi(p)p^{-s}}{1 - p^{-s}}.
\]
This is a ratio of two finite Euler products, meromorphic on $\mathbb{C}$.
Since $|\chi(p)| = 1$, the zeros of $1 - \chi(p)p^{-s} = 0$ satisfy
$\re s = 0$; similarly for $1 - p^{-s} = 0$.  Hence $R$ is \emph{holomorphic
and nowhere zero on the entire strip $S$}.

\textbf{Step 3.} Set $\zeta_{\tilde\chi} = \zeta_\chi \cdot R(s)$.  The factor
$R$ replaces the Euler factors at $p \leq P_0$ by the standard ones, so
$\tilde\chi(p) = 1$ for $p \leq P_0$.  Since $R(z_1) \neq 0$ (Step~2), the
zero of $\zeta_\chi$ at $z_1$ is preserved in $\zeta_{\tilde\chi}$.
Thus $P_S(\zeta_{\tilde\chi}) = 0$, while the Euler data matches
$(1, \ldots, 1)$ at all $p \leq P_0$.

For the all-fiber strengthening, replace $R$ by
$R_a(s) = \prod_{p \leq P_0}(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})/(1-a_p p^{-s})$; the argument
is identical.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope limitation]
Corollary~C applies only to the Helson class.  It does \emph{not} apply to the
Selberg class (which imposes the functional equation, the gamma factor, and
Dirichlet coefficient multiplicativity not present in the Helson class), nor to
$\zeta$ itself.  The escape route is any proof method that uses the full
Selberg-class axioms or $\zeta$-specific structure.
\end{remark}

%%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%%--------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{What the barriers say together}

The three main theorems cover complementary aspects of the finite-arithmetic
landscape:

\begin{itemize}
\item B2 and B1 together say: \emph{no finite collection of Li values, however
  large, determines whether all zeros are on the critical line.}  B2 achieves
  this as an exact collision; B1 says the gap cannot be bounded away from zero.

\item H$'$(i) says: \emph{Li values and counting data are not redundant---each
  sees things the other cannot.}  This means one cannot substitute one
  observation type for the other, and a method combining both faces barriers
  from both sides.

\item Corollary~C says: \emph{finite Euler product information also leaves a
  gap}, specifically within the Helson class.
\end{itemize}

Together they suggest that a complete finite-arithmetic proof of RH would need
to use all three types of information simultaneously, and in an essentially
infinite (or growing with $T$) way.

\subsection{Escape routes}

The following methods are \emph{not} blocked by the theorems in this paper:

\begin{enumerate}
\item An unbounded hierarchy of Li tests ($m \to \infty$, all simultaneously).
\item The full Euler product $\prod_p (1-p^{-s})^{-1}$ plus functional equation.
\item Combined Euler-product and Li discipline with growing $K = K(T)$.
\item The Selberg-class axioms (for Corollary~C only).
\item Methods that use the imaginary part of $\log\zeta(1/2+it)$ continuously
  (not just finitely many samples).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Open problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Grow-with-$T$ barriers.} Is there an information obstruction that
  remains valid even as $K = K(T)$ grows with the height parameter $T$?

\item \textbf{Combined Li + Euler barriers.} Does combining $O_{\mathrm{fin}}^{(K)}$
  with the first $\pi(T)$ Euler factors still leave a gap, or does the combined
  observation resolve RH for sufficiently large $K, T$?

\item \textbf{Weil-type barriers.} Do analogues of B2 and H$'$(i) hold for
  continuous Weil-type observations $\hat h(\gamma_n)$ for fixed $h \in C_c^\infty(\RR)$?

\item \textbf{Information hierarchy for the full zero multiset.}  Is there a
  natural chain of observations $O_1 \preceq O_2 \preceq \cdots$ that converges
  to $O_{\mathrm{oracle}}$, and at what level does the chain separate $P = 1$
  from $P = 0$?
\end{enumerate}
