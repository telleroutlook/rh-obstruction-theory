\title[Spectral Exclusions for Riemann Zero Operators]{%
  Spectral Exclusion Results for Operators Whose Spectrum
  Would Equal the Riemann Zero Ordinates}

\author{Lin Tao}
\date{2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We prove two spectral exclusion results and record two supporting
observations about operators $H$ whose spectrum would equal the multiset
$\{\gamma_n\}$ of positive ordinates of the nontrivial zeros of the
Riemann zeta function.

\textbf{Theorem~D.}  No positive classical elliptic
pseudodifferential operator of order $m>0$ on any compact smooth
manifold has spectrum $\{\gamma_n\}$: the Weyl counting law forces a
pure-power heat trace, while the zero ordinate heat sum
$Z_\zeta(t)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}t^{-1}\log(1/t)$ has an unavoidable
logarithmic factor.

\textbf{Theorem~D$'$.}  The exclusion extends to the class~$\Csub$
of operators with log-polyhomogeneous sub-principal symbols,
via a min-max squeeze argument.

\textbf{Lemma~E$'$.}  Within a natural class of odd meromorphic
functions, the first $k$ positive real zeros together with a $J$-jet
at a base point $w_0$ do not identify a reference function $W$: a
one-parameter polynomial-multiplier deformation achieves the same
finite data while differing globally.

\textbf{Proposition~G.}  The canonical Hadamard product $F_d$ with
zeros $\pm d_n$ (Gram levels) is distinct from the completed xi
function $\hat\Xi$ because $\gamma_1\notin\{d_n\}$.

All results are unconditional; none assumes or concludes the Riemann
Hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection*{Setup and motivation}

The nontrivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ in the upper half-plane may be
written as $\rho_n = \beta_n + i\gamma_n$ with $\beta_n\in(0,1)$ and
$\gamma_n>0$ (listed with multiplicity, $0<\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le\cdots$).
The ordinates $\gamma_n$ are unconditionally real; the Riemann
Hypothesis asserts that $\beta_n=\tfrac{1}{2}$ for all $n$.

A programme going back to Hilbert and P\'olya asks whether a
self-adjoint operator $H$ on a Hilbert space can be exhibited with
$\operatorname{spec}(H)=\{\gamma_n\}$.  (Note: because $\gamma_n$ are
already real, self-adjointness alone does not imply RH; one would
additionally need the spectral determinant to encode $\zeta$ in a way
that forces $\beta_n=\tfrac12$.  The present paper studies only the
\emph{spectral matching} question $\operatorname{spec}(H)=\{\gamma_n\}$,
which is a prerequisite for any such programme.)

A complementary programme asks which operator classes are
\emph{provably incapable} of realizing $\{\gamma_n\}$ as a spectrum.
This paper contributes to that programme.

\subsection*{Results}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textbf{D\arabic*})]
  \item[\textbf{D}] (Section~\ref{sec:elliptic}).  Classical elliptic
    pseudodifferential operators on compact manifolds are excluded
    by a heat-trace comparison.
  \item[\textbf{D$'$}] (Section~\ref{sec:logpoly}).  The exclusion
    extends to the class $\Csub$ of operators with
    log-polyhomogeneous sub-principal symbols.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textbf{E$'$})]
  \item (Section~\ref{sec:jet}).  Jet data at a single point do not
    identify an odd meromorphic function within a natural abstract class.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textbf{G})]
  \item (Section~\ref{sec:gram}).  The Gram-level Hadamard product is
    distinct from $\hat\Xi$ by Hadamard uniqueness.
\end{enumerate}

All four results are RH-free; they establish structural constraints
independent of the truth or falsity of RH.

\subsection*{Related work}

Watson--Valentinuzzi~\cite{Watson2026} prove a Weyl-law obstruction
for compact elliptic \emph{differential} operators; Theorem~D
subsumes their result and extends it to all classical elliptic
pseudodifferential operators via H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968}
(Theorem~4.4).  Theorem~D$'$ covers a wider log-polyhomogeneous
symbol class via Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999}.  Connes~\cite{Connes1999}
places zeta zeros in an adelic spectral setting.
Connes--Consani--Moscovici~\cite{CCM2025} construct a Dirac-type
operator $\mathfrak{D}$ on an adelic space and establish a
regularized determinant identity involving a function $\hat\xi(z)$
(defined via a local Weil quadratic form, not to be confused with
the global Riemann xi function) for which showing convergence to the
Riemann $\Xi$ is the open step.  Suzuki~\cite{Suzuki2013} studies
deformations of the Riemann xi-function via a Weil-type quadratic
form; our Lemma~E$'$ is an abstract function-theoretic statement
that does not apply to those specific functions.

\section{Setup and Notation}
\label{sec:setup}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Heat traces and counting functions}

Let $H$ be a non-negative self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent
on a separable Hilbert space, with eigenvalues
$0\le e_1\le e_2\le\cdots$ (listed with multiplicity).  The
\emph{heat trace} and \emph{spectral counting function} are
\[
  Z_H(t)=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH})=\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-e_n t},
  \quad t>0; \qquad
  N_H(\Lambda)=\#\{n:e_n\le\Lambda\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Karamata~\cite{BGT1987}, Theorem~1.7.1]
\label{thm:karamata}
For $\alpha>0$ and $C>0$,
\[
  N_H(\Lambda)\sim C\Lambda^\alpha
  \;\iff\;
  Z_H(t)\sim C\,\Gamma(\alpha+1)\,t^{-\alpha}
  \quad(t\to 0^+).
\]
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The zero ordinate heat sum}

Write the nontrivial zeros in the upper half-plane as
$\rho_n=\beta_n+i\gamma_n$ ($\beta_n\in(0,1)$, $\gamma_n>0$, listed
with multiplicity).  Define $Z_\zeta(t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-\gamma_n t}$.
The Riemann--von Mangoldt formula (Titchmarsh--Heath-Brown~\cite{Titchmarsh1986},
Theorem~9.4) gives
\[
  N_\zeta(T)\;:=\;\#\{n:\gamma_n\le T\}
  \;\sim\;\frac{T\log T}{2\pi}
  \quad(T\to+\infty),
\]
unconditionally.  Applying the Tauberian form of Karamata's theorem
for logarithmically varying functions (\cite{BGT1987}, \S1.7) yields
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:zetaheat}
  Z_\zeta(t)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}\,t^{-1}\log(1/t)
  \quad(t\to 0^+).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Manifold conventions}

All manifolds are closed (compact, without boundary) and smooth unless
otherwise stated.  A \emph{classical elliptic pseudodifferential
operator} of order $m>0$ on a $d$-dimensional manifold $M$ is a
positive self-adjoint operator $H$ with compact resolvent whose
complete symbol is polyhomogeneous of order $m$ and whose principal
symbol $h_m(x,\xi)$ satisfies $h_m(x,\xi)\ge c|\xi|^m$ uniformly
for $|\xi|\ge 1$.

\section{Classical Elliptic Operators Are Excluded}
\label{sec:elliptic}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[D]
\label{thm:D}
Let $M$ be a closed smooth $d$-manifold and $H$ a positive classical
elliptic pseudodifferential operator of order $m>0$ on $M$ with compact
resolvent.  Then $\operatorname{spec}(H)\ne\{\gamma_n\}$ as multisets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the Weyl law (H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968}, Theorem~4.4),
\[
  N_H(\Lambda)\sim C_H\,\Lambda^{d/m}
  \quad(\Lambda\to+\infty),
\]
where
\[
  C_H=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{T^*M}\mathbf{1}_{h_m(x,\xi)\le 1}\,d\xi\,dx\;>\;0.
\]
(For the specific case $h_m(x,\xi)=|\xi|_x^m$ this reduces to
$(2\pi)^{-d}\omega_d\operatorname{Vol}(M)$, but the general formula
is the cosphere integral above.)  By Theorem~\ref{thm:karamata},
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:classheat}
  Z_H(t)\sim C_H\,\Gamma(d/m+1)\,t^{-d/m}
  \quad(t\to 0^+).
\end{equation}

Suppose for contradiction that $\operatorname{spec}(H)=\{\gamma_n\}$.
Then $Z_H(t)=Z_\zeta(t)$ for all $t>0$, so by~\eqref{eq:zetaheat}:
\[
  C_H\,\Gamma(d/m+1)\,t^{-d/m}
  \;\sim\;
  \frac{1}{2\pi}\,t^{-1}\log(1/t).
\]
Let $\alpha=d/m>0$.  As $t\to 0^+$, the ratio of the two sides is
\[
  \frac{C_H\Gamma(\alpha+1)\,t^{-\alpha}}{(1/2\pi)t^{-1}\log(1/t)}
  \;=\;
  2\pi C_H\Gamma(\alpha+1)\,
  \frac{t^{1-\alpha}}{\log(1/t)}.
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item If $\alpha<1$: the numerator $t^{1-\alpha}\to 0$ while the
  denominator $\log(1/t)\to+\infty$, so the ratio $\to 0$;
  the two sides cannot be asymptotic.
\item If $\alpha>1$: $t^{1-\alpha}\to+\infty$ while $\log(1/t)\to+\infty$
  more slowly, so the ratio $\to+\infty$; again the two sides
  cannot be asymptotic.
\item If $\alpha=1$: the ratio becomes
  $2\pi C_H\Gamma(2)/\log(1/t)\to 0$
  as $t\to 0^+$; so $Z_H(t)\sim C_H t^{-1}$ while
  $Z_\zeta(t)\sim(1/2\pi)t^{-1}\log(1/t)$, and
  $Z_H(t)/Z_\zeta(t)\to 0$; again a contradiction.
\end{itemize}
In all three cases the assumption $Z_H=Z_\zeta$ is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:bgvgilkey}
Berline--Getzler--Vergne~\cite{BGV2004} (Theorem~2.30) and
Gilkey~\cite{Gilkey1995} (\S1.7) give heat-kernel asymptotics
for Laplace-type and differential operators; those results
suffice for a restricted version of Theorem~D but do not
cover general classical elliptic pseudodifferential operators.
The full case uses H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968} (Theorem~4.4).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Watson--Valentinuzzi]
Watson and Valentinuzzi~\cite{Watson2026} prove a Weyl-law obstruction
for compact differential operators.  Theorem~D subsumes this and
covers all classical elliptic pseudodifferential operators
via H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968} (Theorem~4.4).
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Escape routes from Theorem~D}

The proof uses the classical Weyl law, which requires: (i) $M$ compact,
(ii) the operator elliptic, (iii) the symbol polyhomogeneous.
Genuine escape routes are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Non-elliptic operators}---principal symbol vanishing on a
  characteristic set can produce non-pure-power counting asymptotics
  (see the counterexample in Section~\ref{sec:logpoly}).
\item \emph{Operators on non-compact manifolds.}
\item \emph{Sub-classical symbol growth}, e.g.\ $|\xi|/\log|\xi|$-type
  symbols; see the discussion in Section~\ref{sec:logpoly}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Extension to Log-Polyhomogeneous Symbols}
\label{sec:logpoly}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{The class \texorpdfstring{$\Csub$}{C-logpoly}}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:csub}
The class $\Csub$ consists of scalar self-adjoint operators $H\ge -C_0$
(some $C_0\ge 0$) with compact resolvent on a closed $d$-manifold $M$
whose complete symbol has the asymptotic expansion
\[
  \sigma(H)(x,\xi)
  \sim
  h_m(x,\xi)
  +
  \sum_{j\ge 1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{K}
    h_{m-j,\ell}(x,\xi)(\log|\xi|)^\ell
  \quad(|\xi|\to\infty),
\]
where each $h_{m-j,\ell}$ is homogeneous of degree $m-j$, and the
principal symbol $h_m$ satisfies $h_m(x,\xi)\ge c|\xi|^m$ for
$|\xi|\ge 1$ (positive classical elliptic principal part).
\end{definition}

The log-polyhomogeneous calculus is developed in
Grubb--Seeley~\cite{GrubbSeeley1995} and Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999}.

\subsection{Form boundedness of the log correction}

The sub-principal log terms constitute an operator $Q=H-P$, where $P$
is the classical elliptic operator with principal symbol $h_m$ and no
log corrections.  For any $\varepsilon>0$, each term
$h_{m-j,\ell}(x,\xi)(\log|\xi|)^\ell$ with $j\ge 1$ belongs to the
symbol class $S^{m-1+\varepsilon}(T^*M)$ (since $|\log|\xi||=O(|\xi|^\varepsilon)$
for every $\varepsilon>0$).  By the Calder\'on--Vaillancourt theorem and
Sobolev interpolation, $Q$ is form-bounded relative to $P$ with bound~$0$:
for every $\delta>0$ there exists $C_\delta>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:formbdd}
  \lvert\langle Qu,u\rangle\rvert
  \;\le\;
  \delta\langle Pu,u\rangle+C_\delta\|u\|^2
  \qquad\forall\,u\in H^{m/2}(M).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, as quadratic forms on $H^{m/2}(M)$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:formineq}
  (1-\delta)P-C_\delta\;\le\; H\;\le\;(1+\delta)P+C_\delta.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[D$'$]
\label{thm:Dprime}
Any $H\in\Csub$ satisfies $N_H(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$, where
$C_H>0$ is the Weyl constant of the principal symbol $h_m$.  In
particular, $\operatorname{spec}(H)\ne\{\gamma_n\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Replace $H$ by $H+C_0+1$ (shifting so that $H+C_0+1\ge 1>0$), which
does not alter the leading asymptotics.  We may therefore assume
$H\ge 1$.  The comparison operator $P$ satisfies $P\ge c>0$ as well,
and~\eqref{eq:formineq} holds.

By the min-max (Courant--Fischer) principle, \eqref{eq:formineq}
implies
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:minmax}
  N_P\!\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda-C_\delta}{1+\delta}\Bigr)
  \;\le\;
  N_H(\Lambda)
  \;\le\;
  N_P\!\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda+C_\delta}{1-\delta}\Bigr),
  \qquad 0<\delta<1.
\end{equation}
(From $H\le(1+\delta)P+C_\delta$: $\lambda_k(H)\le(1+\delta)\lambda_k(P)+C_\delta$,
so $\lambda_k(P)\le(\Lambda-C_\delta)/(1+\delta)$ whenever $\lambda_k(H)\le\Lambda$,
giving the lower bound.  The upper bound is analogous from the left
inequality.)

Since $N_P(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$ (Theorem~D, applied to $P$),
dividing~\eqref{eq:minmax} by $\Lambda^{d/m}$ and letting
$\Lambda\to+\infty$ gives
\[
  C_H(1+\delta)^{-d/m}
  \;\le\;\liminf_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{N_H(\Lambda)}{\Lambda^{d/m}}
  \;\le\;\limsup_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{N_H(\Lambda)}{\Lambda^{d/m}}
  \;\le\;C_H(1-\delta)^{-d/m}.
\]
Since $\delta>0$ is arbitrary, $N_H(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:karamata}, $Z_H(t)\sim C_H\Gamma(d/m+1)t^{-d/m}$,
and the exclusion follows by the same argument as Theorem~D.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999} Theorem~3.7 establishes heat-trace asymptotics of
$\operatorname{Tr}(Ae^{-tP})$ for log-polyhomogeneous $A$ and classical
elliptic $P$; it does not directly give $e^{-tH}$ asymptotics for
log-polyhomogeneous $H$.  Our proof avoids this gap by using the
form-boundedness comparison~\eqref{eq:formineq} and the classical
Weyl law for $P$, together with the min-max principle.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A genuine non-elliptic escape}

The following example shows that log-enhanced heat traces \emph{can}
occur once ellipticity is dropped.

\begin{remark}[Non-elliptic counterexample]
\label{rem:counterex}
Let $\mathbb{T}^4=\mathbb{T}^2_x\times\mathbb{T}^2_y$ and set
$H_{\mathrm{prod}}=(I-\Delta_x)(I-\Delta_y)$.  The spectrum is
$\{(1+|p|^2)(1+|q|^2)\}_{p,q\in\ZZ^2}$, and a standard lattice-point
argument gives $N_{H_{\mathrm{prod}}}(\Lambda)\sim\pi^2\Lambda\log\Lambda$.
(The torus $\mathbb{T}^2=\RR^2/(2\pi\ZZ)^2$ normalization gives integer
Fourier modes.)  By Karamata,
$Z_{H_{\mathrm{prod}}}(t)\sim\pi^2 t^{-1}\log(1/t)$ as $t\to 0^+$.
This operator is \emph{not} elliptic on $\mathbb{T}^4$: its principal
symbol $|\xi|^2|\eta|^2$ vanishes on the characteristic set
$\{\xi=0\}\cup\{\eta=0\}$, which is non-empty.  Hence logarithmically
enhanced heat traces are achievable outside the elliptic class.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The genuine escape class}

To match $Z_\zeta(t)$ one needs $N_H(T)\sim CT\log T$.  A heuristic
that achieves this counting law is the Lambert-W corrected symbol on the
complete circle $S^1$ (both positive and negative Fourier modes):
\[
  g(r)=\frac{4\pi r}{W(2r/e)},
\]
where $W$ is the Lambert-W function ($W(x)e^{W(x)}=x$).  The factor
$4\pi$ (rather than $2\pi$) accounts for the double-sided spectrum
$\{\pm n\}_{n\ge 1}$ on $S^1$: if $g^{-1}(T)\sim\frac{T}{4\pi}\log(T/2)$,
then $N(T)\approx 2g^{-1}(T)\sim\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$, matching the
first term of $N_\zeta(T)$.  Whether a self-adjoint realization with
spectrum exactly $\{\gamma_n\}$ can be constructed from such symbols
remains open.

\section{Jet Non-Identifiability of Odd Meromorphic Functions}
\label{sec:jet}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Setup}

We work with abstract sequences; the results do not reference $\zeta$.

Let $(a_n)_{n\ge 1}$ be a strictly increasing sequence of positive
reals satisfying $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n^{-2}<\infty$.  Define the
Hadamard product
\[
  A(z)=\prod_{n=1}^\infty\Bigl(1-\frac{z^2}{a_n^2}\Bigr),
\]
which converges absolutely and defines an even entire function with
simple real zeros $\pm a_n$.  Let $B$ be an entire function with no
real zeros, $B(0)\ne 0$, and set $\tilde{B}(z)=B(z)B(-z)$ (even,
nonvanishing on $\RR$).  Define
\[
  W(z)=\frac{z\,A(z)}{\tilde{B}(z)},
\]
which is an \emph{odd} meromorphic function of conventional order $\le 1$.

\begin{definition}[Record class]
\label{def:recordclass}
Fix $k\ge 1$, $J\ge 1$, and a base point $w_0\in\RR\setminus\{0\}$
with $W(w_0)\ne 0$ (i.e., $w_0\ne\pm a_n$ for all $n$).  The
\emph{record class} $\mathcal{E}_{k,J}^{\mathrm{mer}}(w_0)$ consists
of all odd meromorphic functions $F$ of conventional order $\le 1$
satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the (finite) poles of $F$ coincide with those of $W$
  (with the same multiplicities);
\item $F$ has positive real zeros at $a_1,\ldots,a_k$
  (matching the first $k$ zeros of $W$);
\item $\lim_{z\to 0}F(z)/z=A(0)/\tilde{B}(0)$;
\item $F^{(j)}(w_0)=W^{(j)}(w_0)$ for $0\le j<J$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[E$'$]
\label{lem:Eprime}
Under the above conditions, for every $c>0$ sufficiently small the
function
\[
  F_c(z)=W(z)\cdot Q_c(z),
  \quad\text{where }Q_c(z)=1+c\,z^2\,(z^2-w_0^2)^{2J},
\]
belongs to $\mathcal{E}_{k,J}^{\mathrm{mer}}(w_0)$ and satisfies
$F_c\ne W$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$Q_c$ is an even entire polynomial, so $F_c=W\cdot Q_c$ is odd.
$F_c$ has the same poles as $W$ (since $Q_c$ is entire).

\emph{Real zeros.}  For $z\in\RR$ and $c>0$: $z^2\ge 0$ and
$(z^2-w_0^2)^{2J}\ge 0$, so $Q_c(z)\ge 1>0$.  Hence $F_c$ has no
real zeros beyond those of $W$; in particular its first $k$ positive
real zeros are $a_1,\ldots,a_k$. \checkmark

\emph{Normalization at 0.}  $Q_c(0)=1$, so
$\lim_{z\to 0}F_c(z)/z=\lim_{z\to 0}W(z)/z\cdot Q_c(0)=A(0)/\tilde{B}(0)$. \checkmark

\emph{Jet condition.}  At $z=w_0$: $(z^2-w_0^2)^{2J}$ has a zero of
order $2J$ (since $(z^2-w_0^2)$ vanishes simply at $w_0\ne 0$), and
$w_0^2\ne 0$, so $(Q_c-1)=c\,z^2(z^2-w_0^2)^{2J}$ vanishes to order
$2J$ at $w_0$.  Since $W(w_0)\ne 0$,
$F_c-W=W\cdot(Q_c-1)$ also vanishes to order $2J$ at $w_0$, giving
$F_c^{(j)}(w_0)=W^{(j)}(w_0)$ for $0\le j<J$. \checkmark

\emph{Global non-agreement.}  $Q_c\ne 1$ for $c\ne 0$, so $F_c\ne W$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope restriction -- mandatory]
\label{rem:scope}
Lemma~E$'$ applies only to abstract odd meromorphic functions with
the prescribed structure.  It does \emph{not} apply to the actual
Riemann $\xi$-function $\Xi$ or to the Suzuki family $W(a,\theta;z)$:
instantiating the conditions with the actual zero set of $\Xi$ without
independently proving those conditions would import RH-equivalent
information.  The lemma is a statement about an abstract class.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the record class]
\label{rem:trivial}
The record class $\mathcal{E}_{k,J}^{\mathrm{mer}}(w_0)$ is wide
enough that the one-parameter polynomial deformation $F_c$ is
available by inspection.  Lemma~E$'$ shows that any observation
strategy reading only the first $k$ zeros and a $J$-jet at $w_0$
cannot identify $W$ within this class.  To obtain a result with
more analytic content one would need to impose additional global
constraints (e.g., fixed complete zero density, prescribed growth
rate, or a functional equation).
\end{remark}

\section{Gram-Level Hadamard Product}
\label{sec:gram}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Gram points}

The Riemann--Siegel theta function is
\[
  \vartheta(t)=\im\log\Gamma\!\Bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}{2}\Bigr)-\tfrac{t}{2}\log\pi,
\]
satisfying $\vartheta(t)=\tfrac{t}{2}\log\tfrac{t}{2\pi}-\tfrac{t}{2}
-\tfrac\pi8+O(1/t)$ as $t\to+\infty$.  The \emph{Gram points}
$d_n$ ($n\ge 1$) are defined by $\vartheta(d_n)=(n-1)\pi$.  The
first few values are
\[
  d_1\approx 17.8456,\quad
  d_2\approx 23.1703,\quad
  d_3\approx 27.6702,
\]
and $d_n\sim 2\pi n/\log n$ for large $n$ (so $\sum_n d_n^{-2}<\infty$).
The first zero ordinate is $\gamma_1\approx 14.1347$.
Note that
\[
  \gamma_1\approx 14.1347 < d_1\approx 17.8456,
\]
so $\gamma_1\notin\{d_n\}_{n\ge 1}$.

\subsection{The canonical Gram Hadamard product}

Since $\sum_n d_n^{-2}<\infty$, the canonical Hadamard product
\[
  F_d(z)=\prod_{n=1}^\infty\Bigl(1-\frac{z^2}{d_n^2}\Bigr)
\]
converges absolutely and defines an even entire function of conventional
order $\le 1$, with simple real zeros $\pm d_n$ and $F_d(0)=1$.

Recall the completed xi function $\hat\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}
\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$; set $\hat\Xi(z)=\hat\xi(\tfrac12+iz)$, an even
entire function whose positive real zeros are exactly $\{\gamma_n\}$.

\begin{proposition}[G]
\label{prop:G}
$F_d\ne\hat\Xi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\hat\Xi(\gamma_1)=0$ by construction.  Since $\gamma_1\notin\{d_n\}$,
each factor $(1-\gamma_1^2/d_n^2)\ne 0$, so
$F_d(\gamma_1)=\prod_{n=1}^\infty(1-\gamma_1^2/d_n^2)\ne 0$.
Hence $F_d(\gamma_1)\ne\hat\Xi(\gamma_1)$, giving $F_d\ne\hat\Xi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~G follows from Hadamard uniqueness: two entire functions
of order $\le 1$ with different zero sets are distinct.
Since $\gamma_1 < d_1$, the zero sets $\{d_n\}$ and $\{\gamma_n\}$
are disjoint, so $F_d \ne \hat\Xi$ even modulo a nonvanishing
entire factor.  No operator-theoretic framework is required.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to CCM]
\label{rem:ccm}
Connes--Consani--Moscovici~\cite{CCM2025} construct an explicit
Dirac-type operator $\mathfrak{D}$ and study its regularized
determinant.  That construction uses spectrum related to primes and
adelic structure, not the Gram sequence $\{d_n\}$; Proposition~G
does not bear on the CCM programme.
\end{remark}

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorems~D and~D$'$ close off the classical elliptic pseudodifferential
regime on compact manifolds.  The obstruction is the Weyl law: classical
elliptic operators produce pure-power heat traces, incompatible with the
log-enhanced $Z_\zeta(t)\sim(1/2\pi)t^{-1}\log(1/t)$.  Theorem~D$'$
shows this obstruction is stable under log-polyhomogeneous lower-order
perturbations.  The $\mathbb{T}^4$ counterexample shows that once
ellipticity is dropped the log-enhancement is achievable.

Lemma~E$'$ is a function-theoretic statement: within the abstract record
class, finitely many zeros and a finite jet cannot pin down $W$ globally.
Its record class is intentionally minimal; strengthening it (e.g., by
imposing a functional equation or a fixed complete zero density) would
give a more substantive result.

Proposition~G records that the Gram-level Hadamard product $F_d$ and the
completed xi function $\hat\Xi$ are distinct entire functions, with
$\gamma_1$ as an explicit distinguishing point.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Can the CCM normalization step be completed?  Propositions~G does
  not obstruct this, as CCM uses a different operator class.
\item Can a self-adjoint operator with $|\xi|/\log|\xi|$-type symbol on
  some manifold realize $\{\gamma_n\}$ as its spectrum?
\item What is the minimal analytic condition on a symbol class that
  permits $N_H(T)\sim CT\log T$ while retaining a functional calculus?
\item Can Lemma~E$'$ be strengthened by imposing a functional equation
  or prescribed zero density on the record class?
\end{enumerate}
