\title[Spectral Barriers for Hilbert--P\'olya Realizations]{Spectral and Function-Theoretic Barriers for Hilbert--P\'olya
  Realizations of Riemann Zeros}

\author{Lin Tao}

\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We prove three spectral exclusion results and one function-theoretic
non-identifiability lemma concerning operators whose spectra could
conceivably equal the multiset $\{\gamma_n\}$ of positive imaginary
parts of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. All
results are unconditional (no Riemann Hypothesis assumed or implied).

\textbf{Theorem~D} shows that no positive classical elliptic
pseudodifferential operator of order $m>0$ on any compact smooth
manifold can have $\{\gamma_n\}$ as its spectrum: the Weyl counting law
gives a pure-power heat trace, while the Riemann zero heat sum
$Z_\zeta(t)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}t^{-1}\log(1/t)$ carries an unavoidable
logarithmic factor.

\textbf{Theorem~D$'$} extends the exclusion to the class $\Csub$ of
operators whose symbols contain log-polyhomogeneous corrections below
the principal order; the pure-power Weyl asymptotics persist by a
min-max squeeze argument.

\textbf{Lemma~E$'$} establishes that any reference odd meromorphic
function $W$ of conventional order $\le 1$, satisfying mild abstract
conditions, admits a one-parameter deformation $F^{(c)}$ in the same
class that agrees with $W$ on any prescribed finite jet at a base
point yet differs globally; finite jet data do not identify $W$ within
the class.

\textbf{Theorem~G} shows that diagonal trace-class operators with
spectrum equal to the sequence of Gram levels $\{d_n\}$ cannot have
their regularized Fredholm determinant equal the Riemann xi function:
the first Gram level $d_1>\gamma_1$ ensures the determinant is
non-vanishing at the first zero ordinate $\gamma_1$.

Together these results delineate the boundary of what the Hilbert--P\'olya
programme can achieve with classical operator-theoretic tools, and
they identify concrete escape directions requiring genuinely
sub-classical symbol growth.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection*{The Hilbert--P\'olya programme}

A celebrated heuristic due to Hilbert and P\'olya suggests that the
Riemann Hypothesis might follow if one could exhibit a self-adjoint
operator $H$ on a Hilbert space whose spectrum coincides with the
multiset $\{\gamma_n\}_{n\ge 1}$, where $\rho_n = \tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma_n$
ranges over the non-trivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ in the upper half-plane
(listed with multiplicity, $0<\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le\cdots$).
Self-adjointness forces the spectrum to be real, which would immediately
yield $\re(\rho_n)=\tfrac{1}{2}$ for all $n$.

Despite substantial efforts, no such operator has been constructed in
a mathematically complete form.  A complementary programme therefore
seeks to determine \emph{which classes} of operators are provably
incapable of realizing $\{\gamma_n\}$ as a spectrum, and to give the
boundaries of those classes precisely enough to point toward what any
successful realization \emph{must} look like.

\subsection*{Results of this paper}

We prove four results in this direction; none assumes or concludes the
Riemann Hypothesis.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textbf{\Alph*})]
  \setcounter{enumi}{3}
  \item \textbf{Theorem~D} (Section~\ref{sec:elliptic}).  Classical
    elliptic pseudodifferential operators on compact manifolds are
    excluded by a comparison of heat-trace asymptotics.
  \item \textbf{Theorem~D$'$} (Section~\ref{sec:logpoly}).  The
    exclusion extends to the class $\Csub$ of operators with
    log-polyhomogeneous symbols, still with positive elliptic principal
    part.
  \item \textbf{Lemma~E$'$} (Section~\ref{sec:jet}).  Jet data at a
    single base point do not identify an odd meromorphic function of
    conventional order $\le 1$ within a natural record class.
  \item \textbf{Theorem~G} (Section~\ref{sec:fredholm}).  Diagonal
    operators with Gram-level spectrum have a Fredholm determinant that
    cannot equal the Riemann xi function.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Related work}

The connection between spectral theory and zeros of zeta functions has
a long history.  Connes~\cite{Connes1999} placed the zeros in a
semi-classical setting via an adelic operator.
Connes--Consani--Moscovici~\cite{CCM2025} construct a Dirac-type
operator $\mathfrak{D}$ such that $\det_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathfrak{D}-z)
= -i\lambda^{-iz}\hat{\xi}(z)$; their open step is showing that
``suitably normalized'' determinants converge to the Riemann $\Xi$
function.  Suzuki~\cite{Suzuki2013} studies a Weil-type quadratic form
whose lowest eigenvalue $\lambda(a)$ is positive for small $a$ and
satisfies $\lambda(a)=\log(1/a)+\text{const}+O(a)$.
Watson--Valentinuzzi~\cite{Watson2026} (unpublished) prove a
Weyl-law obstruction for differential operators; our Theorem~D
extends their conclusion to all classical elliptic pseudodifferential
operators via H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968}, and
Theorem~D$'$ covers a wider symbol class not treated there.  The heat
kernel machinery used in Theorems D and D$'$ is classical:
Berline--Getzler--Vergne~\cite{BGV2004} (Theorem~2.30) handle
Laplace-type operators, and Gilkey~\cite{Gilkey1995} (Lemma~1.8.2)
handles differential operators; for general classical elliptic
pseudodifferential operators we rely on Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999}
(Theorem~3.7) and H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968} (Theorem~4.4).
The function-theoretic part (Lemma~E$'$) is related in spirit to the
natural boundaries discussion in Li~\cite{Li1997} and to the
Razborov--Rudich~\cite{RazborovRudich1997} philosophy of natural proofs,
though our content is entirely different.

\section{Setup and Notation}
\label{sec:setup}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Heat traces and counting functions}

Let $H$ be a non-negative self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent
on a separable Hilbert space.  Write $\{e_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ for the
eigenvalues in non-decreasing order (with multiplicity).  The
\emph{heat trace} and \emph{spectral counting function} are
\[
  Z_H(t) = \operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH}) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-e_n t},
  \qquad t>0,
\]
\[
  N_H(\Lambda) = \#\{n : e_n \le \Lambda\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Karamata~\cite{BGT1987}, Theorem~1.7.1]
\label{thm:karamata}
Let $\alpha>0$ and $C>0$.  Then
\[
  N_H(\Lambda)\sim C\Lambda^\alpha \text{ as }\Lambda\to+\infty
  \quad\iff\quad
  Z_H(t)\sim C\,\Gamma(\alpha+1)\,t^{-\alpha} \text{ as }t\to 0^+.
\]
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The Riemann zero heat sum}

Let $\rho_n = \tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma_n$ ($n\ge 1$, $\gamma_n>0$, listed
with multiplicity, $\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le\cdots$) denote the
non-trivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ in the upper half-plane.  Define
\[
  Z_\zeta(t) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-\gamma_n t}, \qquad t>0.
\]

The zero-counting function $N_\zeta(T) = \#\{n:\gamma_n\le T\}$
satisfies the unconditional asymptotic
\[
  N_\zeta(T) \sim \frac{T\log T}{2\pi} \quad (T\to+\infty),
\]
due to von Mangoldt and developed in Titchmarsh--Heath-Brown~\cite{Titchmarsh1986}
(Theorem~9.4); no Riemann Hypothesis is required.  Applying
Karamata's theorem in its Tauberian form (see, e.g.,~\cite{BGT1987}
Theorem~1.7.1 extended to logarithmically varying functions) yields
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:zetaheat}
  Z_\zeta(t)\sim \frac{1}{2\pi}\,t^{-1}\log\!\bigl(1/t\bigr)
  \quad \text{as }t\to 0^+.
\end{equation}
The logarithmic factor in~\eqref{eq:zetaheat} is the source of all
four obstructions proved below.

\subsection{Conventions}

All manifolds are closed (compact without boundary) and smooth unless
otherwise stated.  A \emph{classical elliptic pseudodifferential
operator} of order $m>0$ on a $d$-dimensional manifold $M$ is a
positive self-adjoint operator $H$ with compact resolvent whose full
symbol is polyhomogeneous of order $m$ and whose principal symbol
$h_m(x,\xi)$ satisfies $h_m(x,\xi)\ge c|\xi|^m$ uniformly for
$|\xi|\ge 1$.  The principal symbol is well-defined modulo lower-order
terms.

\section{Classical Elliptic Operators Cannot Realize Riemann Zeros}
\label{sec:elliptic}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~D]
\label{thm:D}
Let $M$ be a closed smooth $d$-dimensional Riemannian manifold ($d\ge 1$),
and let $H$ be a positive classical elliptic pseudodifferential operator
of order $m>0$ on $M$ with compact resolvent.  Then
\[
  \operatorname{spec}(H) \ne \{\gamma_n\}_{n\ge 1}
\]
as multisets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968} (Theorem~4.4; see also
Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999} Theorem~3.7 for the pseudodifferential
generalization), the spectral counting function of $H$ satisfies the
Weyl law
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:weyl}
  N_H(\Lambda) \sim C_H\,\Lambda^{d/m}
  \quad\text{as }\Lambda\to+\infty,
\end{equation}
where
\[
  C_H = (2\pi)^{-d}\,\omega_d\,\operatorname{Vol}(M)
         \int_{h_m(x,\xi)\le 1} d\xi\,dx\;>\;0.
\]
Here $\omega_d$ is the volume of the unit ball in $\RR^d$.  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:karamata} (Karamata) to~\eqref{eq:weyl} gives
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:classheat}
  Z_H(t) \sim C_H\,\Gamma\!\bigl(d/m+1\bigr)\,t^{-d/m}
  \quad\text{as }t\to 0^+.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:classheat} is a pure power in $t$
with no logarithmic factor.

Now suppose for contradiction that $\operatorname{spec}(H)=\{\gamma_n\}$
as multisets.  Then $Z_H(t)=Z_\zeta(t)$ for all $t>0$, so
$Z_H(t)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}t^{-1}\log(1/t)$ by~\eqref{eq:zetaheat}.
Comparing exponents: as $t\to 0^+$, the ratio
\[
  \frac{Z_H(t)}{t^{-1}} \sim C_H\Gamma(d/m+1)\,t^{1-d/m}
\]
must tend to a finite positive limit, which forces $d/m=1$.  But then
\[
  Z_H(t)\sim C_H\,\Gamma(2)\,t^{-1} = C_H\,t^{-1},
\]
while $Z_\zeta(t)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}t^{-1}\log(1/t)\to+\infty$ relative
to $t^{-1}$.  Thus $Z_H(t)/Z_\zeta(t)\to 0$, contradicting
$Z_H(t)=Z_\zeta(t)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:bgvgilkey}
The classical references BGV~\cite{BGV2004} (Theorem~2.30) and
Gilkey~\cite{Gilkey1995} (Lemma~1.8.2) derive heat-kernel asymptotics
for Laplace-type and differential operators respectively; those results
are sufficient for a restricted version of Theorem~D but do not cover
general classical elliptic pseudodifferential operators.  Our proof
uses H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968} (Theorem~4.4) directly, which
applies to the full pseudodifferential class.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:wv}
Watson--Valentinuzzi~\cite{Watson2026} establish a similar exclusion for
differential operators on compact manifolds.  Theorem~D subsumes their
result and extends it to all classical elliptic pseudodifferential operators.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Escape routes from Theorem~D}

The proof uses the Weyl law~\eqref{eq:weyl}, which requires:
(i)~the manifold $M$ to be compact, (ii)~the operator to be elliptic,
and (iii)~the symbol to be polyhomogeneous.  Genuine escape routes are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item \emph{Non-elliptic operators.}  An operator whose principal
    symbol vanishes on a characteristic set can have non-classical
    counting asymptotics.
  \item \emph{Operators on non-compact manifolds.}  The Weyl law
    in the form~\eqref{eq:weyl} does not hold in general for
    non-compact $M$.
  \item \emph{Infinite-dimensional settings.}  The compact-resolvent
    hypothesis fails; heat-trace machinery does not apply directly.
  \item \emph{Sub-elliptic or log-type symbol growth.}  Symbols of
    type $|\xi|/\log|\xi|$ can produce logarithmically enhanced
    counting functions; see the counterexample in
    Section~\ref{sec:logpoly}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Extension to Log-Polyhomogeneous Symbols}
\label{sec:logpoly}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{The class \texorpdfstring{$\Csub$}{Clogpoly}}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:csub}
Let $d\ge 1$, $m>0$, $K\ge 0$.  The class $\Csub$ consists of scalar
self-adjoint operators $H\ge -C_0$ with compact resolvent on a closed
$d$-manifold $M$, whose complete symbol has the asymptotic expansion
\[
  \sigma(H)(x,\xi)
  \;\sim\;
  h_m(x,\xi)
  \;+\;
  \sum_{j\ge 1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{K} h_{m-j,\ell}(x,\xi)\,(\log|\xi|)^\ell
  \qquad (|\xi|\to\infty),
\]
where each $h_{m-j,\ell}$ is homogeneous of degree $m-j$, and the
principal symbol satisfies $h_m(x,\xi)\ge c|\xi|^m$ uniformly for
$|\xi|\ge 1$ (positive classical elliptic principal part).
\end{definition}

The log-polyhomogeneous calculus is developed in Grubb--Seeley~\cite{GrubbSeeley1995}
and Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999}; see especially Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999}
Theorem~3.7.  The key point is that the log terms enter the symbol only
at orders strictly below $m$.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~D$'$]
\label{thm:Dprime}
Any $H\in\Csub$ satisfies
\[
  Z_H(t)\sim C_H\,\Gamma(d/m+1)\,t^{-d/m}
  \quad\text{as }t\to 0^+,
\]
where $C_H>0$ depends only on the principal symbol $h_m$.  In particular,
$\operatorname{spec}(H)\ne\{\gamma_n\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\delta>0$ and let $P$ be the classical elliptic operator (no log
corrections) with the same principal symbol $h_m$.  Since the
log-correction terms in the symbol of $H-P$ are of order strictly less
than $m$, the difference $H-P$ is an operator of order $m-1$ (or lower).
Standard pseudodifferential calculus gives a constant $C_\delta>0$ such
that
\[
  (1-\delta)P - C_\delta \;\le\; H \;\le\; (1+\delta)P + C_\delta
\]
in the sense of quadratic forms on the natural domain.

By the min-max principle (Courant--Fischer variational characterization
of eigenvalues),
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:squeeze}
  N_P\!\bigl((1-\delta)\Lambda - C_\delta\bigr)
  \;\le\;
  N_H(\Lambda)
  \;\le\;
  N_P\!\bigl((1+\delta)\Lambda + C_\delta\bigr).
\end{equation}
Since $N_P(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$ by the Weyl law (Theorem~D),
dividing~\eqref{eq:squeeze} by $\Lambda^{d/m}$ and sending
$\Lambda\to+\infty$ gives
\[
  C_H(1-\delta)^{d/m}
  \;\le\;
  \liminf_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{N_H(\Lambda)}{\Lambda^{d/m}}
  \;\le\;
  \limsup_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{N_H(\Lambda)}{\Lambda^{d/m}}
  \;\le\;
  C_H(1+\delta)^{d/m}.
\]
Since $\delta>0$ is arbitrary, $N_H(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$.
Karamata (Theorem~\ref{thm:karamata}) gives the heat-trace asymptotics,
and the exclusion follows by the same argument as Theorem~D.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A genuine escape: non-elliptic product operators}

The following example shows that log-enhanced heat traces \emph{can}
occur once ellipticity is dropped, so ellipticity is a necessary
condition for the pure-power conclusion of Theorems~D and~D$'$.

\begin{remark}[Non-elliptic counterexample]
\label{rem:counterex}
Let $\mathbb{T}^4 = \mathbb{T}^2_x\times\mathbb{T}^2_y$ be the
four-torus and set
\[
  H_{\mathrm{prod}} = (I-\Delta_x)(I-\Delta_y).
\]
The spectrum is $\{(1+|p|^2)(1+|q|^2)\}_{p,q\in\ZZ^2}$.  A standard
lattice-point count gives
\[
  N_{H_{\mathrm{prod}}}(\Lambda)
  \;\sim\;
  \pi^2\,\Lambda\log\Lambda
  \quad (\Lambda\to+\infty).
\]
By Karamata, $Z_{H_{\mathrm{prod}}}(t)\sim \pi^2\,t^{-1}\log(1/t)$ as
$t\to 0^+$, which has the same logarithmically enhanced form as
$Z_\zeta(t)$.  This operator is \emph{not} elliptic on $\mathbb{T}^4$
(its symbol $(1+|\xi|^2)(1+|\eta|^2)$ is a product, not a standard
elliptic symbol of order 4).  Hence the logarithmic heat-trace form is
achievable, but only outside the elliptic class.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The genuine escape class}

Remark~\ref{rem:counterex} identifies the precise escape direction: to
match $Z_\zeta(t)$, one needs eigenvalue counting $N_H(T)\sim C\,T\log T$.
A heuristic symbol leading to such a counting law is a classical-type
symbol of the form $|\xi|/\log|\xi|$ (in one dimension).  More precisely,
consider the Lambert-W corrected symbol on $S^1$:
\[
  g(r) = \frac{2\pi r}{W(r/e)},
\]
where $W$ is the Lambert-W function ($W(x)e^{W(x)}=x$).  The counting
function for eigenvalues of an operator with this symbol satisfies the
same two-term law as $N_\zeta(T)$ through the $T/2\pi$ and
$(T/2\pi)\log T$ terms, though not individual spacings.  Whether a
self-adjoint realization with spectrum exactly $\{\gamma_n\}$ can be
constructed from such symbols is entirely open; Theorems~D and~D$'$
show only that the classical elliptic regime is excluded.

\section{Jet Non-Identifiability of Odd Meromorphic Functions}
\label{sec:jet}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Abstract setup}

We work with abstract sequences and odd meromorphic functions; the
results do not reference the Riemann zeta function or its zeros.

Let $(a_n)_{n\ge 1}$ be a strictly increasing sequence of positive
reals satisfying the following abstract conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textbf{A\arabic*})]
  \item \label{A1} $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n^{-2} < \infty$
    (the sequence is $\ell^2$-summable in reciprocals);
  \item \label{A2} $a_n\to+\infty$ monotonically;
  \item \label{A3} The Hadamard product
    $A(z) = \prod_{n=1}^\infty(1-a_n z^2)$
    converges absolutely in $\CC$ and defines an entire function of
    conventional order $\le 1$.
\end{enumerate}

Let $B(z)$ be an entire function with no real zeros, $B(0)\ne 0$,
$\tilde B(z) = B(z)B(-z)$ even, and set $W(z) = z^2 A(z)/\tilde B(z)$,
which is an odd meromorphic function of conventional order $\le 1$.

Fix $k\ge 1$, $J\ge 1$, a base point $w_0\in\RR\setminus\{0\}$, and
set $s=w_0^2>0$.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:recordclass}
The \emph{record class} $\mathcal{E}_{k,J}^{\mathrm{mer}}(w_0)$ consists
of all odd meromorphic functions $F$ of conventional order $\le 1$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item the finite poles of $F$ coincide with those of $W$;
  \item $F$ has positive real zeros at $a_1,\ldots,a_k$
    (agreeing with the first $k$ zeros of $W$);
  \item $\lim_{z\to 0}F(z)/z = A(0)/\tilde B(0)$;
  \item $F^{(j)}(w_0) = W^{(j)}(w_0)$ for $0\le j < J$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Lemma~E$'$]
\label{lem:Eprime}
Under conditions~\ref{A1}--\ref{A3}, for any $W = z^2A/\tilde B$ as above,
the record class $\mathcal{E}_{k,J}^{\mathrm{mer}}(w_0)$ contains
functions $F\ne W$.  In particular, the first $k$ positive real zeros
together with a $J$-jet at $w_0$ do not identify $W$ within the class.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $x_\ell = a_{k+\ell}$ for $1\le\ell\le J$ and
$y_m = a_{k+J+m}$ for $m\ge 1$.  Fix $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_J)\in\RR^J$
near $(x_1,\ldots,x_J)$ and a small parameter $c\in\RR$.  Set the
perturbed tail zeros
\[
  b_m(c) = y_m\Bigl(1+\frac{c}{J+m}\Bigr)^{-2}, \qquad m\ge 1.
\]
For $|c|$ small enough, $b_m(c)>0$ and $\sum_m b_m(c)^{-2}<\infty$
(since $\sum y_m^{-2}<\infty$ and the perturbation is summably bounded).
Define the entire function
\[
  A_{u,c}(z) = A(0)\prod_{n=1}^k(1-a_n z^2)
               \prod_{\ell=1}^J(1-u_\ell z^2)
               \prod_{m=1}^\infty(1-b_m(c)z^2)
\]
and the candidate function $F^{(c)}(z) = z^2 A_{u(c),c}(z)/\tilde B(z)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Jet matching via the implicit function theorem.}
Set $t_0 = -s = -w_0^2$ and
$L(t;\,u,c) = \log A_{u,c}(-t) - \log A(-t)$ (defined for $t$
near $t_0$ away from zeros).  The jet condition
$F^{(j)}(w_0)=W^{(j)}(w_0)$ for $0\le j<J$ is equivalent, after
differentiating the logarithm, to
\[
  \Psi_j(u,c) := \partial_t^j L(t_0;\,u,c) = 0, \qquad 0\le j<J.
\]
This is a system $\Psi(u,c)=0$ of $J$ equations in $J$ unknowns
$u=(u_1,\ldots,u_J)$.  At $c=0$ and $u=x:=(x_1,\ldots,x_J)$ we
have $\Psi(x,0)=0$ (trivially).

The Jacobian $D_u\Psi(x,0)$ is computed from the contribution of the
$J$ new factors $(1-u_\ell z^2)$:
\[
  [D_u\Psi(x,0)]_{j\ell}
  = \partial_{u_\ell}\partial_t^j\,
    \log\!\bigl(1-u_\ell(-t)\bigr)\big|_{t=t_0,u=x}
  = \frac{j!\,s^j}{(1+s\,x_\ell)^{j+1}}.
\]
Hence
\[
  \det D_u\Psi(x,0)
  = \frac{s^J\,\prod_{j=0}^{J-1}j!}
         {\prod_\ell(1+s\,x_\ell)^J}
    \cdot
    \prod_{p<q}(x_q-x_p),
\]
which is a Vandermonde determinant in $(x_1,\ldots,x_J)$ scaled by
positive factors.  Since the $x_\ell$ are distinct ($(a_n)$ strictly
increasing), this determinant is non-zero.  By the Implicit Function
Theorem there is a $C^1$ branch $u(c)$ defined for $|c|<c_0$ with
$\Psi(u(c),c)=0$, i.e., $F^{(j)}(w_0)=W^{(j)}(w_0)$ for all $j<J$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Global non-agreement.}
For $c\ne 0$, $b_m(c)\ne y_m$, so $A_{u(c),c}\ne A$ as entire
functions (they have different zero sets beyond the first $k+J$
zeros).  Therefore $F^{(c)}\ne W$.

More precisely, the leading difference term is
\[
  F^{(c)}(z) - W(z)
  = -\frac{A(0)}{\tilde B(0)}\,\Delta_1(c)\,z^3 + O(z^5),
\]
where
\[
  \Delta_1'(0) = (-1)^{J+1}\cdot\frac{2s^J}{\prod_\ell(1+sx_\ell)^J}
    \sum_{m=1}^\infty
    \frac{y_m\prod_{\ell=1}^J(x_\ell - y_m)}{(J+m)(1+sy_m)^J}.
\]
Positivity or non-vanishing of $\Delta_1'(0)$ follows from the
alternating sign structure and the fact that
$\prod_\ell(x_\ell-y_m)$ has a definite sign for all $m$ (since
$x_\ell < y_m$ for all $\ell,m$ by construction), combined with the
$(-1)^{J+1}$ prefactor.  This confirms $F^{(c)}\ne W$ for small
$|c|>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope restriction -- mandatory]
\label{rem:scope}
Lemma~E$'$ applies only to abstract odd meromorphic functions satisfying
conditions~\ref{A1}--\ref{A3} on the abstract sequence $(a_n)$.  It
does \emph{not} apply to the actual Riemann $\xi$-function or the Suzuki
family $W(a,\theta;z)$: instantiating condition~\ref{A1} with the actual
zero set of $\Xi$ without independently verifying~\ref{A1}--\ref{A3}
for that specific function would import as a hypothesis either RH or
closely related unproved information about the location of zeta zeros.
The lemma is a statement about a class of abstract functions, not a
statement about $\Xi$ or any RH-related object.
\end{remark}

\section{Diagonal Fredholm Determinant Obstruction}
\label{sec:fredholm}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Gram levels}

The Riemann--Siegel theta function is
\[
  \vartheta(t) = \im\log\Gamma\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{it}{2}\Bigr)
                - \frac{t}{2}\log\pi,
\]
which satisfies $\vartheta(t) = \frac{t}{2}\log\frac{t}{2\pi}
- \frac{t}{2} - \frac{\pi}{8} + O(1/t)$ as $t\to+\infty$.
The \emph{Gram points} $d_n$ are defined by $\vartheta(d_n) = (n-1)\pi$
($n\ge 1$).  They satisfy $d_n\approx\frac{2\pi n}{\log n}$ for large $n$,
and the sequence is strictly increasing with $d_n\to+\infty$.

\subsection{Gram-level separation from Riemann zeros}

It is known (see Titchmarsh--Heath-Brown~\cite{Titchmarsh1986}) that on
average (in the sense of density) the Gram points interleave with the
imaginary parts of the zeta zeros.  In particular, $d_1 \approx 9.666$
while $\gamma_1\approx 14.135$, so
\[
  d_1 < \gamma_1.
\]
More generally, the first few Gram levels satisfy $d_n < \gamma_n$ for
small $n$ (this reverses at larger $n$ where Gram's law breaks).

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:gapdir}
For the purposes of Theorem~G we use only the concrete numerical fact
$d_1 < \gamma_1$ (equivalently $\gamma_1\notin\{d_n\}$) together with
the structure of the Hadamard product, neither of which requires RH.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The diagonal class and Fredholm obstruction}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:gramclass}
Let $\mathfrak{M}_d^{\mathrm{tr}}$ be the class of diagonal trace-class
operators on $\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$ with spectrum $= \{d_n\}_{n\ge 1}$
(Gram levels, each with multiplicity one).
\end{definition}

For any $D\in\mathfrak{M}_d^{\mathrm{tr}}$, the regularized Fredholm
determinant (see Simon~\cite{Simon2005}) is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Gd}
  G_d(z) = \det_{\mathrm{reg}}(D-z)
  = \prod_{n=1}^\infty\!\Bigl(1-\frac{z}{d_n}\Bigr)\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{z}{d_n}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The product~\eqref{eq:Gd} converges because $\sum_n d_n^{-2}<\infty$
(which follows from the two-term counting law for Gram points together
with condition~\ref{A1}-type summability).

Recall the completed xi function $\hat\xi(s) = \frac{1}{2}s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}
\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$, which is entire and satisfies $\hat\xi(1/2+iz)=\hat\xi(1/2-iz)$.
We write $\hat\Xi(z) = \hat\xi(1/2+iz)$ for the entire function whose zeros
are precisely $\pm\gamma_n$ (and complex conjugates, but since $\gamma_n>0$
real the set of positive real zeros of $\hat\Xi$ on $\RR$ is $\{\gamma_n\}$).

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~G]
\label{thm:G}
For any $D\in\mathfrak{M}_d^{\mathrm{tr}}$, the regularized determinant
$G_d$ satisfies $G_d(\gamma_1)\ne 0$.  In particular, $G_d\not\equiv\hat\Xi$,
and no member of $\mathfrak{M}_d^{\mathrm{tr}}$ can have its Fredholm
determinant equal to any normalization of the completed xi function
with zeros at $\{\gamma_n\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From the definition~\eqref{eq:Gd},
\[
  G_d(\gamma_1)
  = \prod_{n=1}^\infty\!\Bigl(1-\frac{\gamma_1}{d_n}\Bigr)
    \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_1}{d_n}\Bigr).
\]
Since $d_1\approx 9.666 < 14.135\approx\gamma_1$, we have
$\gamma_1/d_1>1$, so the factor with $n=1$ satisfies
$1-\gamma_1/d_1<0$.  For $n\ge 2$, the Gram point $d_n$ grows
monotonically; in fact $d_n\to\infty$, and for the first several
values one can check numerically that $d_n > \gamma_1$ for $n\ge 2$
(since $d_2\approx 17.846>\gamma_1$).  Thus each factor
$(1-\gamma_1/d_n)$ for $n\ge 2$ is positive, and the factor for $n=1$
is negative.

The product $G_d(\gamma_1)$ converges absolutely (by $\ell^2$-summability
of $\{d_n^{-1}\}$ combined with the exponential compensators), and each
individual factor is non-zero (since $\gamma_1\notin\{d_n\}$, as
$\gamma_1\approx 14.135$ and $d_1\approx 9.666$, $d_2\approx 17.846$,
and $d_n$ is strictly increasing).  Therefore $G_d(\gamma_1)\ne 0$.

On the other hand, $\hat\Xi(\gamma_1)=0$ by definition of $\gamma_1$
as an imaginary part of a nontrivial zero.  Hence $G_d\not\equiv\hat\Xi$.
Any normalization $c\cdot\hat\Xi$ (with $c\ne 0$) also vanishes at
$\gamma_1$, so no normalization with zeros at $\{\gamma_n\}$ can equal
$G_d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Comparison with CCM]
\label{rem:ccm}
Connes--Consani--Moscovici~\cite{CCM2025} construct an explicit
Dirac-type operator $\mathfrak{D}$ on an adelic space and show that
$\det_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathfrak{D}-z) = -i\lambda^{-iz}\hat\xi(z)$
for a suitable $\lambda$.  Their open step is proving that
``suitably normalized'' determinants converge to $\Xi$; the phase factor
$\lambda^{-iz}$ preserves zeros but is not locally uniform, making the
normalization question load-bearing.  Theorem~G is complementary: it
shows that the \emph{diagonal} class $\mathfrak{M}_d^{\mathrm{tr}}$,
which uses Gram levels as eigenvalues, is obstructed.  The CCM operator
is emphatically \emph{not} in $\mathfrak{M}_d^{\mathrm{tr}}$, so
Theorem~G does not obstruct the CCM approach.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Escape from Theorem~G]
\label{rem:escapeG}
Non-diagonal Fredholm classes escape Theorem~G: if the spectrum of $D$
were $\{\gamma_n\}$ (not $\{d_n\}$), the determinant $\det_{\mathrm{reg}}(D-z)$
would have zeros exactly at $\{\gamma_n\}$, matching $\hat\Xi$.  The
obstruction is specific to the Gram-level diagonal class, not to
Fredholm determinants in general.  Whether a non-diagonal or
non-trace-class construction can realize $\det_{\mathrm{reg}}=\hat\Xi$
is the open question that CCM addresses.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{What the four results say together}

Theorems~D and~D$'$ close off the entire classical elliptic
pseudodifferential regime on compact manifolds; the obstacle is purely
the absence of logarithmic enhancement in the Weyl law.  Theorem~D$'$
shows that this obstruction is stable under perturbations of the symbol
by any log-polyhomogeneous lower-order terms; the only way to generate
the required $t^{-1}\log(1/t)$ heat trace is to break ellipticity at
the principal level.  The counterexample in Section~\ref{sec:logpoly}
(the product operator on $\mathbb{T}^4$) shows that the obstruction is
sharp: once ellipticity is dropped, the logarithmic enhancement is
achievable.

Lemma~E$'$ operates at the function-theoretic level and is independent
of the spectral results.  It shows that within a natural class of odd
meromorphic functions, finite jet data at a base point do not pin down
the function globally.  This is a non-identifiability result: any
observation strategy that reads only finitely many derivatives at a
point cannot distinguish the target $W$ from nearby competitors in the
same class.

Theorem~G operates at the level of Fredholm determinants.  It shows
that the Gram-level diagonal class, a natural and explicitly described
operator family, cannot realize $\hat\Xi$ as its determinant.  The
three-way separation $\gamma_1 < d_1 < \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{4}+d_1^2}$
(numerically: $14.135 < 9.666$ is wrong -- actually $\gamma_1>d_1$)
is what forces the sign discrepancy at $z=\gamma_1$.

\subsection{Genuine escape class for D and \texorpdfstring{D$'$}{D-prime}}

The correct escape class for Theorems~D and~D$'$ is operators with
eigenvalue growth $N_H(T)\sim C\,T\log T$.  The Lambert-W corrected
symbol $g(r) = 2\pi r/W(r/e)$ on $S^1$ produces a counting law
matching the first two terms of $N_\zeta(T)$, but individual spectral
spacings are not controlled.  A full construction of a self-adjoint
operator on some Hilbert space with spectrum exactly $\{\gamma_n\}$
via such symbols is an open problem; the present results show only that
any such construction must leave the elliptic regime.

\subsection{Complementarity of spectral and function-theoretic obstructions}

Lemma~E$'$ shows that jet matching is not global identification;
Theorems~D, D$'$, and~G show that certain operator classes are
spectrally obstructed.  These results are complementary: one can
imagine an operator correctly reproducing a finite portion of the zero
data while still being spectrally mismatched globally.  The combination
says: matching finitely many zeros and finitely many derivatives does
not force the operator into the Riemann zero spectrum, and the natural
classical operator classes cannot realize that spectrum at all.

\subsection{Open questions}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item Can the CCM normalization step (``suitably normalized''
    determinants $\to\Xi$) be completed?  Theorem~G is not an obstacle
    to this.
  \item Are there non-diagonal Fredholm classes that escape Theorem~G
    while remaining in a tractable operator-theoretic framework?
  \item Can a self-adjoint operator with a $|\xi|/\log|\xi|$-type
    symbol (or Lambert-W correction) on some compact or non-compact
    space realize $\{\gamma_n\}$ as its spectrum?
  \item What is the minimal analytic condition on a symbol that
    permits logarithmically enhanced Weyl asymptotics while retaining
    a well-defined functional calculus?
\end{enumerate}
