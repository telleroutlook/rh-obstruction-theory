\title[Spectral Exclusion Results for Riemann Zero Operators]{%
  Spectral Exclusion Results for Operators Whose Eigenvalue
  Multiset Would Equal the Riemann Zero Ordinates}

\author{Lin Tao}
\date{2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We prove two spectral exclusion results showing that no operator in
a natural geometric class can have its eigenvalue multiset equal to
$\{\gamma_n\}$, the positive ordinates of the nontrivial zeros of
the Riemann zeta function.

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:D}.}  No positive classical elliptic
pseudodifferential operator of order $m>0$ on any closed smooth
manifold of dimension $d\ge 1$ has eigenvalue multiset $\{\gamma_n\}$:
the Weyl counting law forces a pure-power leading term in the heat
trace, while the zero ordinate heat sum
$Z_\zeta(t)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}t^{-1}\log(1/t)$
has an unavoidable logarithmic factor.

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:Dprime}.}  The exclusion extends to the class~$\Csub$
of operators whose symbol has a classical positive elliptic principal
part and strictly lower-order log-polyhomogeneous corrections,
via a min-max squeeze argument.

Both results are unconditional; neither assumes nor concludes the
Riemann Hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{Setup and motivation}

The nontrivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ in the upper half-plane may be
written as $\rho_n = \beta_n + i\gamma_n$ with $\beta_n\in(0,1)$ and
$\gamma_n>0$ (listed with multiplicity, $0<\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le\cdots$).
The ordinates $\gamma_n$ are unconditionally real; the Riemann
Hypothesis asserts that $\beta_n=\tfrac{1}{2}$ for all $n$.

A programme going back to Hilbert and P\'olya seeks a self-adjoint
operator $H$ on a Hilbert space---with a natural geometric or analytic
origin---whose spectrum equals $\{\gamma_n\}$.  Even granted a formal
construction, spectral matching alone does not imply RH: one would
additionally require an analytic identity linking $H$ to a determinant
or trace formula that forces $\beta_n=\tfrac12$ (see
Berry--Keating~\cite{BerryKeating1999} for a survey of this programme).
The present paper asks which naturally defined operator classes are
\emph{provably incapable} of achieving the spectral match
$\operatorname{spec}_{\times}(H)=\{\gamma_n\}$.

\subsection*{Results}

The labels D and D$'$ follow the numbering of the parent project from
which this paper extracts the two spectral exclusion results.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textbf{\Alph*})]
  \setcounter{enumi}{3}
  \item[\textbf{D}] (Section~\ref{sec:elliptic}).  Classical elliptic
    pseudodifferential operators on closed manifolds of dimension $d\ge 1$
    are excluded by a heat-trace comparison.
  \item[\textbf{D$'$}] (Section~\ref{sec:logpoly}).  The exclusion
    extends to the class $\Csub$ of operators with a classical positive
    elliptic principal symbol and strictly lower-order
    log-polyhomogeneous corrections.
\end{enumerate}

Both results are RH-free; they establish structural constraints
independent of the truth or falsity of RH.

\subsection*{Related work}

Watson--Valentinuzzi~\cite{Watson2026} prove a Weyl-law obstruction
for compact elliptic \emph{differential} operators; Theorem~\ref{thm:D}
establishes the same obstruction for classical elliptic
pseudodifferential operators of any order $m>0$ via the
pseudodifferential Weyl law (H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968}).
The Laplace-type and differential-operator results of
Berline--Getzler--Vergne~\cite{BGV2004} and Gilkey~\cite{Gilkey1995}
cover a restricted subclass; see Remark~\ref{rem:bgvgilkey}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:Dprime} extends the result to the subclass of the
log-polyhomogeneous symbol calculus of Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999} in
which the principal symbol is classical and positive elliptic and all
logarithmic terms appear only at strictly lower orders.
Berry and Keating~\cite{BerryKeating1999} survey the Hilbert--P\'olya
approach and the relationship between eigenvalue asymptotics and zero
counting.
Connes~\cite{Connes1999} places zeta zeros in a noncommutative
geometric setting.  In Connes--Consani--Moscovici~\cite{CCM2025},
scaling operators $D_{\log}^{(\lambda,N)}$ on $L^2([\lambda^{-1},\lambda],d^\times u)$
satisfy (Theorem~5.10) the regularized-determinant identity
$\det_{\mathrm{reg}}(D_{\log}^{(\lambda,N)}-z)=-i\lambda^{-iz}\hat{\xi}_{\lambda,N}(z)$,
where $\xi_{\lambda,N}$ is the lowest eigenfunction of the finite-truncation Weil
form~$QW_\lambda^N$---not the Riemann completed-zeta function
$\Xi(t):=\xi(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)$, which arises in the separate convergence result
$\hat{k}_\lambda\to\Xi$ for $|\operatorname{Im}z|<\tfrac{1}{2}$ (Lemma~7.3).
The open steps (\S8) are establishing that the continuum lowest eigenfunction
$\xi_\lambda$ (the $N\to\infty$ limit) is simple and even, and that the cut-off
kernel $k_\lambda$ converges sufficiently to $\xi_\lambda$ to recover~$\Xi$;
the normalization is the load-bearing unresolved ingredient.
Theorems~D and~D$'$ do not obstruct the CCM approach, since the CCM
operators belong to a different class.

\subsection{Heat traces and counting functions}

Let $H$ be a non-negative self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent
on a separable Hilbert space, with eigenvalues
$0\le e_1\le e_2\le\cdots$ listed with multiplicity.
We refer to this listed sequence as the \emph{eigenvalue multiset}
of $H$ and write it $\operatorname{spec}_{\times}(H)$.
For the positive elliptic operators we consider, the heat semigroup
$e^{-tH}$ is trace class for all $t>0$ (Seeley~\cite{Seeley1967}),
and the \emph{heat trace} and \emph{spectral counting function} are
\[
  Z_H(t)=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH})=\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-e_n t},
  \quad t>0; \qquad
  N_H(\Lambda)=\#\{n:e_n\le\Lambda\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Karamata~\cite{BGT1987}]
\label{thm:karamata}
Let $H$ be as above.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{(Pure-power case, \cite{BGT1987} Theorem~1.7.1)}
For $\alpha>0$ and $C>0$,
\[
  N_H(\Lambda)\sim C\Lambda^\alpha
  \;\iff\;
  Z_H(t)\sim C\,\Gamma(\alpha+1)\,t^{-\alpha}
  \quad(t\to 0^+).
\]
\item \emph{(Log-varying case, \cite{BGT1987} \S1.7.2, Theorem~1.7.1)}
\[
  N_H(\Lambda)\sim C\Lambda\log\Lambda
  \;\iff\;
  Z_H(t)\sim C\,t^{-1}\log(1/t)
  \quad(t\to 0^+).
\]
(Take $\rho=1$, $c=C$, $\ell(x)=\log x$, $\Gamma(2)=1$ in Theorem~1.7.1.)
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The zero ordinate heat sum}

Write the nontrivial zeros in the upper half-plane as
$\rho_n=\beta_n+i\gamma_n$ ($\beta_n\in(0,1)$, $\gamma_n>0$, listed
with multiplicity).  Define $Z_\zeta(t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-\gamma_n t}$.
The Riemann--von Mangoldt formula
(Titchmarsh--Heath-Brown~\cite{Titchmarsh1986}, Theorem~9.4) gives
\[
  N_\zeta(T)\;:=\;\#\{n:\gamma_n\le T\}
  \;\sim\;\frac{T\log T}{2\pi}
  \quad(T\to+\infty),
\]
unconditionally.  Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:karamata}(ii) yields
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:zetaheat}
  Z_\zeta(t)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}\,t^{-1}\log(1/t)
  \quad(t\to 0^+).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Manifold conventions}

All manifolds are closed (compact, without boundary) and smooth unless
otherwise stated.  A \emph{classical elliptic pseudodifferential
operator} of order $m>0$ on a closed $d$-dimensional manifold $M$
($d\ge 1$) is a positive self-adjoint operator $H$ with compact
resolvent whose complete symbol is polyhomogeneous of order $m$ and
whose principal symbol $h_m(x,\xi)$ satisfies $h_m(x,\xi)\ge c|\xi|^m$
uniformly for $|\xi|\ge 1$.
Throughout, all operators act on \emph{scalar} functions (sections of
the trivial line bundle over $M$); for operators on sections of a
vector bundle with positive-definite endomorphism-valued principal
symbol $p_m(x,\xi)$, the Weyl constant takes the general form
$(2\pi)^{-d}\!\int_{T^*M}\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\mathbf{1}_{p_m(x,\xi)\le 1}\bigr]\,d\xi\,dx$
(indicator applied spectrally); the exclusion arguments go through unchanged.

\section{Classical Elliptic Operators Are Excluded}
\label{sec:elliptic}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{mainthm}
\label{thm:D}
Let $M$ be a closed smooth manifold of dimension $d\ge 1$ and $H$ a
positive classical elliptic pseudodifferential operator of order $m>0$
on $M$ with compact resolvent.  Then
$\operatorname{spec}_{\times}(H)\ne\{\gamma_n\}$.
\end{mainthm}

\begin{proof}
By the pseudodifferential Weyl law
(H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968}, Theorem~4.4; for general order $m>0$
reduce to order~$1$ via $H^{1/m}$, which is a classical elliptic
$\Psi$DO of order~$1$ by Seeley's complex-powers
construction (Shubin~\cite{Shubin2001}, \S11, Theorem~11.2;
see also~\cite{Seeley1967}), with $N_H(\Lambda)=N_{H^{1/m}}(\Lambda^{1/m})$),
\[
  N_H(\Lambda)\sim C_H\,\Lambda^{d/m}
  \quad(\Lambda\to+\infty),
\]
where, for a scalar operator on $M$,
\[
  C_H=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{T^*M}\mathbf{1}_{h_m(x,\xi)\le 1}\,d\xi\,dx\;>\;0.
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:karamata}(i),
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:classheat}
  Z_H(t)\sim C_H\,\Gamma(d/m+1)\,t^{-d/m}
  \quad(t\to 0^+).
\end{equation}

Suppose for contradiction that $\operatorname{spec}_{\times}(H)=\{\gamma_n\}$.
Then $Z_H(t)=Z_\zeta(t)$ for all $t>0$, so
by~\eqref{eq:zetaheat} and~\eqref{eq:classheat}:
\[
  C_H\,\Gamma(d/m+1)\,t^{-d/m}
  \;\sim\;
  \frac{1}{2\pi}\,t^{-1}\log(1/t).
\]
Set $\alpha=d/m>0$.  The ratio of the two sides is
\[
  2\pi C_H\Gamma(\alpha+1)\,
  \frac{t^{1-\alpha}}{\log(1/t)},
\]
which tends to $0$ if $\alpha<1$, to $+\infty$ if $\alpha>1$, and to
$0$ if $\alpha=1$.  In all cases the ratio does not tend to $1$,
contradicting $Z_H(t)=Z_\zeta(t)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:bgvgilkey}
The heat-kernel results of Berline--Getzler--Vergne~\cite{BGV2004}
(Theorem~2.30) and Gilkey~\cite{Gilkey1995} (\S1.7) cover
Laplace-type and differential operators; those results suffice for
a restricted version of Theorem~\ref{thm:D} but do not cover general
classical elliptic pseudodifferential operators.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Watson--Valentinuzzi]
Watson and Valentinuzzi~\cite{Watson2026} prove a Weyl-law obstruction
for compact elliptic differential operators.  Theorem~\ref{thm:D} establishes
the same obstruction for classical elliptic pseudodifferential
operators of any order $m>0$.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Escape routes from Theorem~\ref{thm:D}}

The proof uses the classical Weyl law, which requires: (i) $M$ compact,
(ii) the operator elliptic, (iii) the symbol polyhomogeneous.
Escape routes include:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Non-elliptic operators}---principal symbol vanishing on a
  characteristic set can produce non-pure-power counting asymptotics
  (see the counterexample in Section~\ref{sec:logpoly}).
\item \emph{Operators on non-compact manifolds.}
\item \emph{Sub-classical symbol growth}, e.g.\ $|\xi|/\log|\xi|$-type
  symbols; see the discussion in Section~\ref{sec:logpoly}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The class \texorpdfstring{$\Csub$}{C-logpoly}}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:csub}
Let $M$ be a closed smooth manifold of dimension $d\ge 1$, $m>0$,
$K\in\mathbb{N}_0$.
The class $\Csub$ consists of \emph{formally symmetric} scalar
pseudodifferential operators $H$ on $C^\infty(M)$---meaning
$\langle Hu,v\rangle=\langle u,Hv\rangle$ for all $u,v\in C^\infty(M)$---that
are \emph{semi-bounded}: $\langle Hu,u\rangle\ge -C_0\|u\|^2$ for some
$C_0\ge 0$ and all $u\in C^\infty(M)$.
The complete symbol in any local coordinate chart satisfies the asymptotic expansion
\[
  \sigma(H)(x,\xi)
  \sim
  h_m(x,\xi)
  +
  \sum_{j\ge 1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{K}
    h_{m-j,\ell}(x,\xi)\,\chi(\xi)\,(\log|\xi|)^\ell
  \quad(|\xi|\to\infty),
\]
where $h_m$ is a \emph{real-valued}, smooth, positively homogeneous symbol
of degree $m$, $\chi\in C^\infty(\RR^d)$ with $\chi(\xi)=0$ for $|\xi|\le 1$
and $\chi(\xi)=1$ for $|\xi|\ge 2$, and:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item each $h_{m-j,\ell}$ is smooth and positively homogeneous of
    degree $m-j$ in $\xi$ for $|\xi|\ge 1$, satisfying
    \[
      |\partial_x^\alpha\partial_\xi^\beta h_{m-j,\ell}(x,\xi)|
      \le C_{\alpha\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{m-j-|\beta|}
    \]
    for all multiindices $\alpha,\beta$;
  \item the $N$th remainder
    \[
      \sigma(H)-h_m-\sum_{1\le j\le N}\sum_\ell h_{m-j,\ell}\,\chi\,(\log|\xi|)^\ell
    \]
    lies in the standard H\"ormander class $S^{m-N}_{1,0}(T^*U)$
    for each coordinate chart $U$
    \emph{(in the sense of Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999}, Definition~3.1)};
  \item the principal symbol satisfies $h_m(x,\xi)\ge c|\xi|^m$ for
    $|\xi|\ge 1$ and all $x\in M$
    (\emph{positive classical elliptic principal part}).
\end{enumerate}
By the G\r{a}rding inequality for classical elliptic $\Psi$DOs
(Shubin~\cite{Shubin2001}, \S6, Theorem~6.2) applied to $h_m$,
the shifted form $q_{H,+}[u]:=\langle(H+C_0+1)u,u\rangle$, initially
on $C^\infty(M)$, satisfies $q_{H,+}[u]\ge c\,\|u\|^2_{H^{m/2}(M)}$
and closes to the Sobolev form domain $\mathrm{Dom}(q_{H,+})=H^{m/2}(M)$.
The first representation theorem for quadratic forms
(Reed--Simon~\cite{RS1975}, Theorem~X.23) yields a unique self-adjoint
operator, the Friedrichs extension of~$H$, associated with the closed
form~$q_{H,+}$;
by elliptic regularity (Shubin~\cite{Shubin2001}, \S7),
$\mathrm{Dom}(H)=H^m(M)$ and the resolvent is compact.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Form boundedness of the strictly lower-order log corrections}

Let $P_0$ be the classical elliptic operator of order~$m$ on~$M$ with
principal symbol~$h_m$ and no log-polyhomogeneous terms, obtained by
global pseudodifferential quantization of~$h_m$ (via a partition of
unity, cf.~H\"ormander~\cite{Hormander1968}) and symmetrization.
Fix $b\ge 0$ so that $P:=P_0+bI\ge 1$.
Then $P\in\Psi^m_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)$ with $\sigma_m(P)=h_m$ and
$\mathrm{Dom}(q_P)=H^{m/2}(M)$.

By the G\r{a}rding inequality for classical elliptic $\Psi$DOs
(Shubin~\cite{Shubin2001}, \S6, Theorem~6.2) applied to the
elliptic principal symbol $h_m$, there exist $c_0>0$ and $C_1<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:garding}
  \langle Pu,u\rangle \;\ge\; c_0\,\|u\|^2_{H^{m/2}(M)} - C_1\,\|u\|^2,
  \qquad u\in C^\infty(M).
\end{equation}
Since $H\in\Csub$ has the same principal symbol $h_m$,
Definition~\ref{def:csub} gives $\mathrm{Dom}(q_H)=H^{m/2}(M)$ as well.
Write $Q_0:=H-P_0$ and $Q:=H-P=Q_0-bI$.
On $C^\infty(M)$, $q_Q[u]=q_{Q_0}[u]-b\|u\|^2$; the $-b\|u\|^2$ term
is absorbed directly into~$C_\delta\|u\|^2$ in the form bound below.
We show $Q_0\in\Psi^{m-1+\varepsilon}_{1,0}(M)$ for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$,
as follows.

For any $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, since $K<\infty$ and $j\ge 1$,
each symbol term $h_{m-j,\ell}(x,\xi)\chi(\xi)(\log|\xi|)^\ell$ satisfies
the $S^{m-1+\varepsilon}_{1,0}$ symbol estimates: for $|\xi|\ge 1$ and all
$0\le\ell\le K$, the estimate $|(\log|\xi|)^\ell|\le C_{\varepsilon,K}|\xi|^\varepsilon$
together with the derivative estimates on $h_{m-j,\ell}$ and the
product rule give
\[
  |\partial_x^\alpha\partial_\xi^\beta[h_{m-j,\ell}\chi(\xi)(\log|\xi|)^\ell]|
  \le C'_{\alpha\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{m-1+\varepsilon-|\beta|}.
\]
The $N$th remainder is controlled by Definition~\ref{def:csub}(ii).
Hence $Q_0\in\Psi^{m-1+\varepsilon}_{1,0}(M)$ for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$.

For $u\in H^{m/2}(M)$, the form estimate proceeds as follows.
If $m>1-\varepsilon$, the Sobolev interpolation inequality on $M$
gives, for every $\delta>0$:
\[
  \|u\|_{H^{(m-1+\varepsilon)/2}(M)}^2
  \;\le\;
  \delta\,\|u\|_{H^{m/2}(M)}^2+C_\delta\,\|u\|_{L^2(M)}^2,
\]
since $(m-1+\varepsilon)/2<m/2$.  By~\eqref{eq:garding},
$\|u\|^2_{H^{m/2}}\le c_0^{-1}(\langle Pu,u\rangle+C_1\|u\|^2)$,
so the interpolation can be absorbed into $\langle Pu,u\rangle$.
The pseudodifferential Sobolev
mapping theorem gives
$Q_0\colon H^{(m-1+\varepsilon)/2}(M)\to H^{-(m-1+\varepsilon)/2}(M)$,
so
\[
  |q_{Q_0}[u]|=|\langle Q_0 u,u\rangle|
  \le C'\|u\|_{H^{(m-1+\varepsilon)/2}}^2
  \le C'\bigl(\delta\|u\|_{H^{m/2}}^2+C_\delta\|u\|_{L^2}^2\bigr).
\]
If instead $m\le 1-\varepsilon$, then $Q_0\in\Psi^{m-1+\varepsilon}_{1,0}$
with $m-1+\varepsilon\le 0$ is $L^2$-bounded~\cite{CalderonVaillancourt1972},
so $|q_{Q_0}[u]|\le C\|u\|_{L^2}^2\le
C(\delta\|u\|_{H^{m/2}}^2+C_\delta\|u\|_{L^2}^2)$.
In both cases, $|q_Q[u]|\le|q_{Q_0}[u]|+b\|u\|^2$, so adjusting constants
and using~\eqref{eq:garding} yields:
for every $\delta>0$,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:formbdd}
  |q_Q[u]|
  \;\le\;
  \delta\langle Pu,u\rangle+C_\delta\|u\|^2
  \qquad\forall\,u\in H^{m/2}(M).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, as quadratic forms on their common domain $H^{m/2}(M)$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:formineq}
  (1-\delta)P-C_\delta\;\le\; H\;\le\;(1+\delta)P+C_\delta.
\end{equation}

\begin{mainthmprime}
\label{thm:Dprime}
Any $H\in\Csub$ satisfies
$N_H(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$ as $\Lambda\to\infty$,
where $C_H>0$ is the Weyl constant of the principal symbol $h_m$.
In particular, $\operatorname{spec}_{\times}(H)\ne\{\gamma_n\}$.
\end{mainthmprime}

\begin{proof}
Write $a=C_0+1$.  Replace $H$ by $\tilde H=H+a$, which satisfies
$\tilde H\ge 1>0$ and $N_{\tilde H}(\Lambda)=N_H(\Lambda-a)$, so
$N_H(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$ is equivalent to
$N_{\tilde H}(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$.
The difference $\tilde Q:=\tilde H-P=Q+aI$ satisfies
$|q_{\tilde Q}[u]|\le|q_Q[u]|+a\|u\|^2$,
so~\eqref{eq:formbdd} holds for $\tilde Q$ with $C_\delta$ replaced by
$C_\delta+a$.  Since $\tilde Q=Q+aI$, \eqref{eq:formbdd} gives
$|q_{\tilde Q}[u]|\le\delta\langle Pu,u\rangle+(C_\delta+a)\|u\|^2$,
so the form inequality for $\tilde H$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:formineqshift}
  (1-\delta)P-(C_\delta+a)\;\le\;\tilde H\;\le\;(1+\delta)P+(C_\delta+a),
  \quad \tilde H\ge 1.
\end{equation}

By the min-max (Courant--Fischer) principle, if $A\le B$ as quadratic
forms on a common domain, then $\lambda_k(A)\le\lambda_k(B)$.
Applying this to~\eqref{eq:formineqshift}:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:minmax}
  N_P\!\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda-(C_\delta+a)}{1+\delta}\Bigr)
  \;\le\;
  N_{\tilde H}(\Lambda)
  \;\le\;
  N_P\!\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda+(C_\delta+a)}{1-\delta}\Bigr),
  \qquad 0<\delta<1.
\end{equation}
(Lower bound: $\tilde H\le(1+\delta)P+(C_\delta+a)$ implies
$\lambda_k(P)\le(\Lambda-(C_\delta+a))/(1+\delta)\Rightarrow\lambda_k(\tilde H)\le\Lambda$;
upper bound: $(1-\delta)P-(C_\delta+a)\le\tilde H$ implies
$\lambda_k(\tilde H)\le\Lambda\Rightarrow\lambda_k(P)\le(\Lambda+(C_\delta+a))/(1-\delta)$.)

Since $P$ is a positive classical elliptic pseudodifferential operator
of order $m$, the pseudodifferential Weyl law~\cite{Hormander1968}
gives $N_P(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$.
Dividing~\eqref{eq:minmax} by $\Lambda^{d/m}$ and letting
$\Lambda\to+\infty$ gives
\[
  C_H(1+\delta)^{-d/m}
  \;\le\;\liminf_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{N_{\tilde H}(\Lambda)}{\Lambda^{d/m}}
  \;\le\;\limsup_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{N_{\tilde H}(\Lambda)}{\Lambda^{d/m}}
  \;\le\;C_H(1-\delta)^{-d/m}.
\]
Since $\delta>0$ is arbitrary, $N_{\tilde H}(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$.
Since $N_H(\Lambda)=N_{\tilde H}(\Lambda+a)$, it follows that
$N_H(\Lambda)\sim C_H\Lambda^{d/m}$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:karamata}(i),
$Z_{\tilde H}(t)\sim C_H\Gamma(d/m+1)t^{-d/m}$.
Since $Z_H(t)=e^{at}Z_{\tilde H}(t)$ and $e^{at}\to 1$ as $t\to 0^+$,
we also have $Z_H(t)\sim C_H\Gamma(d/m+1)t^{-d/m}$,
and the exclusion follows by the same argument as Theorem~\ref{thm:D}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Lesch~\cite{Lesch1999} Theorem~3.7 establishes the resolvent kernel
expansion for log-polyhomogeneous operators, from which
equations~(3.18)--(3.20) derive heat-trace asymptotics for
$\operatorname{Tr}(Ae^{-tP})$ with log-polyhomogeneous $A$ and
classical elliptic $P$.  This does not directly give $Z_H(t)$
asymptotics when $H$ itself is log-polyhomogeneous; the present proof
avoids this gap via the form-boundedness comparison and the classical
Weyl law for $P$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A non-elliptic escape}

The following example shows that log-enhanced heat traces \emph{can}
occur once ellipticity is dropped.

\begin{remark}[Non-elliptic counterexample]
\label{rem:counterex}
Let $\mathbb{T}^4=\mathbb{T}^2_x\times\mathbb{T}^2_y$ and set
$H_{\mathrm{prod}}=(I-\Delta_x)(I-\Delta_y)$, with eigenvalues
$(1+|p|^2)(1+|q|^2)$ for $(p,q)\in\ZZ^2\times\ZZ^2$
(integer Fourier modes for $\mathbb{T}^2=\RR^2/(2\pi\ZZ)^2$).
We show $N_{H_{\mathrm{prod}}}(\Lambda)\sim\pi^2\Lambda\log\Lambda$.

Let $A(X)=\#\{q\in\ZZ^2:1+|q|^2\le X\}=\pi X+O(X^{1/2})$
(standard two-dimensional lattice-point estimate; see
Hardy--Wright~\cite{HardyWright2008}, Theorem~278 for $r_2$, and the
circle bound follows by unit-square area comparison).  Then
\[
  N_{H_{\mathrm{prod}}}(\Lambda)
  =\sum_{\substack{p\in\ZZ^2\\1+|p|^2\le\Lambda}}
  A\!\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda}{1+|p|^2}\Bigr)
  =\pi\Lambda\!\sum_{\substack{p\in\ZZ^2\\1+|p|^2\le\Lambda}}
  \frac{1}{1+|p|^2}+O(\Lambda),
\]
where the error is $O(\Lambda)$ because $A(X)=\pi X+O(X^{1/2})$ and
\[
  \sum_{\substack{p\in\ZZ^2\\1+|p|^2\le\Lambda}}
  \!\!\!\left(\frac{\Lambda}{1+|p|^2}\right)^{\!1/2}
  =\Lambda^{1/2}\!\!\!\sum_{\substack{p\in\ZZ^2\\1+|p|^2\le\Lambda}}
  \!(1+|p|^2)^{-1/2}
  =O(\Lambda)
\]
(the last sum is $O(\Lambda^{1/2})$ by grouping into dyadic shells).

For the main sum, let $r_2(k)=\#\{p\in\ZZ^2:|p|^2=k\}$ and write
$\sum_{1+|p|^2\le\Lambda}(1+|p|^2)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\Lambda\rfloor-1}r_2(k)/(1+k)$.
The circle-problem estimate $\sum_{k\le R}r_2(k)=\pi R+O(R^{1/2})$
and Abel summation give
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\Lambda\rfloor-1}\frac{r_2(k)}{1+k}
  \;\sim\;\pi\log\Lambda.
\]
Hence $N_{H_{\mathrm{prod}}}(\Lambda)\sim\pi^2\Lambda\log\Lambda$.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:karamata}(ii),
$Z_{H_{\mathrm{prod}}}(t)\sim\pi^2 t^{-1}\log(1/t)$ as $t\to 0^+$.
This operator is \emph{not} elliptic on $\mathbb{T}^4$: its principal
symbol $|\xi|^2|\eta|^2$ vanishes on $\{\xi=0\}\cup\{\eta=0\}$.
Hence logarithmically enhanced heat traces are achievable outside the
elliptic class, showing that ellipticity is essential for the
Weyl-law argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:D}.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The escape class}

To match $Z_\zeta(t)\sim(1/2\pi)t^{-1}\log(1/t)$ one needs
$N_H(T)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}T\log T$.
A heuristic model achieving this counting law is the Lambert-$W$
corrected symbol on $S^1$ (both positive and negative Fourier modes):
\[
  g(r)=\frac{4\pi r}{W(2r/e)}\;\text{ for }r\ge 1
\]
(extended smoothly to $r\in[0,1]$ to satisfy the $\Psi$DO symbol
estimates at low frequency), where $W$ is the Lambert-$W$ function
($W(x)e^{W(x)}=x$).  Setting
$g(r)=T$ and solving gives the exact inverse (valid for $T\ge 4\pi/W(2/e)\approx 27.1$,
equivalently for all sufficiently large~$T$)
\[
  g^{-1}(T)=\frac{T}{4\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi e}.
\]
Each eigenvalue $g(n)$ for $n\ge 1$ carries multiplicity $2$
(from Fourier modes $e^{\pm inx}$), so
\[
  N_g(T)=2\bigl\lfloor g^{-1}(T)\bigr\rfloor+O(1)
  \;\sim\;\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi e}
  \;\sim\;\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T.
\]
Whether a self-adjoint operator belonging to a natural symbol class
(with specified decay of symbol derivatives and operator-theoretic
core conditions) can realize $\{\gamma_n\}$ as its eigenvalue multiset
remains open; see Berry--Keating~\cite{BerryKeating1999} for the
broader programme.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}% xref-only
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorems~D and~D$'$ show that no operator in the class of positive
classical elliptic pseudodifferential operators on a closed manifold,
nor in the broader subclass $\Csub$ (classical positive elliptic
principal part, strictly lower-order log-polyhomogeneous corrections),
can have eigenvalue multiset $\{\gamma_n\}$.  The obstruction is the
Weyl law: operators in these classes have a pure-power leading term in
their heat trace, incompatible with
$Z_\zeta(t)\sim(1/2\pi)t^{-1}\log(1/t)$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:Dprime} shows this obstruction is stable under strictly
lower-order log-polyhomogeneous perturbations.
The $\mathbb{T}^4$ counterexample (Remark~\ref{rem:counterex})
shows that ellipticity of the principal symbol is essential for the
Weyl-law argument: without it, logarithmically enhanced heat traces
can occur.

Open questions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Can the CCM open steps---establishing that the lowest eigenvalue
  is simple and even, and that $k_\lambda$ converges to the lowest
  eigenfunction in a suitable sense---be completed, yielding a
  spectral realization of $\Xi$ from the scaling-operator family?
\item Can a self-adjoint operator with $|\xi|/\log|\xi|$-type symbol
  on some manifold realize $\{\gamma_n\}$ as its eigenvalue multiset?
\item For which symbol classes, specified by explicit membership
  conditions (decay of symbol derivatives, ellipticity type, domain
  characterization), does the counting law $N_H(T)\sim CT\log T$ hold?
\end{enumerate}
